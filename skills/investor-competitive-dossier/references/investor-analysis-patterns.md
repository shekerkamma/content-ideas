# Investor analysis patterns

## Evidence states

Use these unless the sector supplies better audited measures:

- **Roadmap:** architecture or target only.
- **Silicon:** working or sampled device.
- **Validation:** named partner or customer evaluation.
- **Production:** repeatable paid deployment with disclosed operating proof.

Never infer a later state from fundraising, hiring, a partnership logo, an evaluation kit, or
an unnamed contract announcement.

## Competitive arenas

Define arenas by buyer job and substitutable budget, not editorial list membership. Test for:

- vertically integrated incumbents;
- merchant incumbents and adjacent startups;
- customer-built/internal alternatives;
- standards or open-source commoditization;
- partner, attach, acquisition, and bundling paths.

## Company teardown

Every teardown answers:

1. Who are the real competitors?
2. Why can this company win?
3. What is independently proven?
4. What evidence is still missing?
5. Which observable event breaks the thesis?

## Valuation discipline

Show valuation beside evidence state, not as proof. Distinguish:

- total funding from latest-round size;
- pre-money from post-money valuation;
- pipeline from backlog;
- signed contracts from recognized revenue;
- company-reported demand from audited financial traction.

Avoid synthetic value/proof ratios when contract quality, ownership, dilution, margins, or
revenue recognition are not disclosed.

## Portfolio roles

Prefer roles to false rankings:

- **Core diligence:** broad attachment plus credible validation/production evidence.
- **Execution diligence:** plausible product with a commercialization, systems, or software gap.
- **Milestone option:** roadmap-stage, narrow attachment, or predominantly vendor-controlled proof.

Position sizing requires price, ownership, fund construction, reserves, and loss tolerance. Do
not invent allocation percentages when those inputs are absent.

## Storyboard quality test

The dossier is shallow if it can be summarized as one slide per company. It is decision-ready
only when it explains market structure, competitive arenas, evidence quality, contradictions,
bull and bear mechanisms, scenarios, proof requests, and what changes the recommendation.
