# Honesty rules

An ad that looks real can mislead. These rules keep the work usable and keep the person out of trouble. Follow them without being asked, and say so when a request runs into one.

## Whose brand is it?

**The person's own product or a client's, with permission**: build it as an ad.

**Someone else's brand** (a practice piece, a tutorial, a pitch): it is a **spec ad**.

- Label it. On screen, small, while any words are up: "Spec ad. Not affiliated with {brand}." If the person wants a clean picture, put the label in the notes and the description instead, and tell them it has to travel with the video.
- Say it in the hand-over notes: not made for, endorsed by or affiliated with the brand.
- Do not present it as official. Do not run it as paid advertising.
- Do not imitate the brand's own campaign so closely that a viewer would think the brand made it.

## Real assets, never fake ones

- Use the real logo file. **Never draw or generate a logo from memory**: a nearly-right logo is a wrong logo.
- Use real product photos as references, and check the result against them character by character.
- Do not let a model invent text on a product. If it does, reject the take.
- Record where every asset came from in `assets/SOURCES.md`.
- Reference photos belong to their photographers. Use them as private references. Do not publish them, and do not put them in a public repository.
- Fonts: open-licence fonts, or ones the person has a licence for.

## Claims and words

- A tagline you write is yours. Say in the notes that it was written for this piece and is not the brand's line.
- No invented numbers, awards, reviews, prices or customer quotes. A counter counts up to a real figure, from a source you can point to.
- A voiceover over real footage uses only facts that are in that footage. If no date is shown, do not state a date.

## People

- Do not add names, titles or quotes to people in footage. Do not put words in a real person's mouth.
- Do not edit faces.
- Generate a real person's likeness only when that person asked for it and supplied their own picture. Otherwise frame the shot so there is no face (cut at the collarbone), or use an invented person.
- An AI voice is an AI voice. Do not present it as a named real person.

## Footage and music

- Footage must be the person's to use. An event's recap belongs to the event: a re-cut of it is a spec piece unless the event asked for it.
- Never change or move the source file. Work on copies.
- Generated music and effects come with the terms of the service that made them. Sounds made with `make_sounds.py` are sine waves and noise and are free to use.

## Following a reference

Take the style, not the content. See `style-and-meta-prompting.md` for what may and may not be carried over.

## Being straight about the work

- Say which parts are real and which are generated.
- Say what you checked and what you could not (you cannot hear or watch playback).
- List known flaws with timecodes.
- Report what it cost, including the takes that were thrown away.
- If a step in this skill was skipped, such as the storyboard approval, say so.

## Publishing

Do not post, upload, schedule or send anything. Hand the file to the person. Publishing is their decision.
