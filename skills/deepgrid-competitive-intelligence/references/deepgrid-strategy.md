# DeepGrid strategy reference

Use this file as the internal strategic baseline. It is supplied by the client and is not independent market evidence.

## Product and evidence position

- DeepGrid Semi Pvt Ltd is an Indian semiconductor startup in Hyderabad building perception silicon for AIS-162 AEBS applications on N2/N3 commercial vehicles.
- Current product: DGS001 FPGA module using AMD Artix-7 XC7A200T. Client-supplied measured claims: YOLOv11n at 40 fps; attention-head latency 24.25 ms.
- Planned product: DeepGrid ASIC. Planned tapeout Q1–mid 2027; earliest qualified volume H1 2028.
- `PLAN FIGURE`: compute BOM Rs 65,000→Rs 1,750; Rs 63,250 reduction.
- `PLAN FIGURE`: kit gross margin 45%→72% at an unchanged Rs 2.50 lakh kit price.
- Documentary client claims: 15 provisional patents, active GeM listing, domestic design/IP ownership.
- Unvalidated planning figure: tapeout capex Rs 20–35 Cr.
- DeepGrid supplies perception—detect, classify, track—not full AEBS. Brake actuation and the AIS-162 safety case remain with the EBS Tier-1 and vehicle OEM.

## Mandate and timing

Client baseline cites G.S.R. 834(E), 11 Nov 2025, making AIS-162 AEBS mandatory for M2/M3/N2/N3:

- New models: 1 Jan 2027.
- Existing models: 1 Oct 2027.
- LDWS AIS-188, DDAWS AIS-184, BSIS AIS-186, and MOIS AIS-187 follow from 1 Oct 2027 / 1 Jan 2028.

Verify regulatory language against the official notification before external use.

Strategic implications:

1. The ASIC is a margin event, not market entry.
2. January 2027 line-fit is treated as lost; target the October 2027 wave and 2028–29 cost-down re-source.
3. Government/PSU/Defence is the current balance-sheet segment.
4. Use braking Tier-1s as the safety-case channel.

## Segment A — Government, PSU, Defence

- Capacity: 50%, now.
- Buyers: STUs, Coal India entities and HEMM contractors, energy/logistics PSUs, defence transport and DPSUs, and municipal heavy fleets.
- Sales cycle: 4–9 months; client deal range Rs 50L–Rs 12 Cr.
- Procurement gates: GeM and Class-I local supplier. Captive/off-road/retrofit use may not require AIS-162 approval; verify per tender and vehicle use.
- Decision maker: GM Mechanical/Engineering or Chief Engineer Fleet who shapes tender criteria.
- Channel: direct + GeM; DPSU primes for defence.
- Message: sovereignty and procurability first; cost second.

## Segment B — N2/N3 CV OEMs

- Capacity: 30%, deferred to 2028–29.
- Named accounts: Tata Motors CV, Ashok Leyland, VECV, Daimler India, Mahindra Truck and Bus, SML Isuzu, Force Motors. Target three design-ins.
- Sales cycle: 18–30 months; client deal range Rs 40–200 Cr/year at volume.
- Required gates: AIS-162, ISO 26262, IATF 16949, AEC-Q100, integration maturity, field evidence, and supply continuity.
- Buying centre: ADAS engineering, purchasing, homologation, and EBS Tier-1.
- Two tracks: Tier-1 partnership and OEM-directed sourcing.
- DeepGrid currently claims two of seven gates: DGS001 A-sample and field/false-positive data obtainable from Segment A/C pilots. Treat even these as client assertions until artifacts are supplied.

## Segment C — Mining, Yard, Aftermarket

- Capacity: 20%, Q1 2027+.
- Buyers: mining/HEMM, ports and terminals, cement bulk fleets, and large tipper/trailer operators.
- Sales cycle: 2–5 months; client deal range Rs 12L–Rs 2.5 Cr.
- Off-road/captive contexts may not require AIS-162; verify use and DGMS/tender requirements.
- Decision maker: Head of HSE or statutory Mine Manager.
- Trigger: liability and incident cost, not road mandate.
- Channel: SI/distributor led; retain control of perception and data stack.

## Hard disqualifiers

1. Decision requires certified ASIC before H1 2028.
2. Customer requires DeepGrid to carry full AIS-162 type-approval responsibility.
3. Free hardware or unfunded custom engineering without committed volume.
4. No vehicle access for pilot fitment within 90 days of contract.
