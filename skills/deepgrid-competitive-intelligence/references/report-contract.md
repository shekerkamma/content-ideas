# Report and presentation contract

## Required competitor report

1. Competitor profile summary.
2. Segment-by-segment threat assessment.
3. Scorecard exploitation, with non-scorable dimensions left null.
4. Hard-disqualifier inversion.
5. Messaging collision map by C-suite, Chief Engineer/SI, and Government Procurement.
6. Strategic white space and falsifiers.
7. Three to five 90-day counter-moves with owner and done-when criterion.
8. Evidence gaps and next monitoring triggers.

## Threat levels

- `NONE`: structurally outside the segment or product scope, supported by evidence.
- `LOW`: some overlap, but a documented structural or execution barrier prevents near-term displacement.
- `MEDIUM`: credible overlap and route to market, with meaningful gaps or uncertain evidence.
- `HIGH`: shipping relevant product plus a credible buyer/channel/procurement path and proof position.
- `NOT_ASSESSABLE`: evidence cannot support a threat judgment.

Never use `NONE` merely because evidence is missing.

## Client-deck handoff

The analytical run must provide:

- validated dossier JSON files;
- evidence ledger;
- normalized comparison matrix;
- allowed-number list separating measured, documentary, plan, and competitor numbers;
- executive threat report;
- claim IDs and source IDs suitable for slide traceability;
- unresolved conflicts and falsifiers.

The presentation stage must then create its own governed contracts and QA evidence. Do not put raw internal validation labels, file paths, or production notes on client-facing slides.
