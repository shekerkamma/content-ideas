# Genspark concept prompt for a Vault-native final deck

Create a 16:9 presentation concept from the supplied validated story and source
material. Treat the supplied reference deck or design specification as the
storyline and semantic design language.

Use assertion-style titles and give every slide one primary structure: a card
grid, comparison, process, architecture flow, matrix, evidence strip, or staged
roadmap. Keep each title to two lines where possible, use no more than five major
objects, and show the implication or next decision on every slide.

Use color to encode stable roles such as control, execution, intelligence, and
caution. Keep the visual language restrained and operational. Avoid generic AI
imagery, fake interfaces, decorative dashboards, unsupported metrics, exposed
credentials, and body text below 12 px.

This deck is an upstream concept for a fully native PowerPoint rebuild. Optimize
the storyline and visual hierarchy, but do not rely on effects that cannot be
translated into native shapes. Keep diagrams structured, labels explicit, and
screenshots limited to exact supplied evidence.

Do not force the content into a predetermined number of slides. Derive the
length from the supplied evidence and coverage matrix. Treat any requested count
as a minimum or planning estimate unless it is explicitly labeled a hard maximum.
Add slides whenever a distinct operating state, proof object, or caveat would
otherwise be omitted or overcrowded.

Before completion:

1. Report the chosen slide count and show that every required evidence row is covered.
2. Check the title sequence as a complete argument.
3. Confirm every factual claim is traceable to supplied material.
4. Keep semantic color meaning consistent.
