01 The read, up front

Fighting them for the control plane is a fight we lose on distribution alone.

They own the vocabulary, the analyst narrative, and the default shortlist position. Their Microsoft Agent 365 integration just took away the “we’re the neutral one” story. So the play is: concede the tower, own the ground floor.

Move 1

Launch HARC AMS as a fixed-price, per-agent managed governance offer with an OT compliance pack.

Move 2

Integrate with AI Control Tower and Agent 365 — instead of positioning against them.

Move 3

Run a CFO-targeted campaign pricing our fixed model against their metered one.

02 Bull case · why they win and we lose

They start inside every account we’d sell to.

Already installed

AI Control Tower isn’t a new procurement — it’s a checkbox on a renewal. Now governs agents on any platform via Action Fabric’s MCP server.

Neutrality gap closing

Deepened integration with Microsoft Agent 365 and Entra Agent ID. The two vendors certify each other — our cross-runtime pitch is now their headline.

Bundling kills pricing

AI folded into core Foundation/Advanced/Prime packaging. A separate governance line now looks like duplicate spend. Microsoft reinforces at $15/user/mo in E7.

Public proof we lack

Rolls-Royce — asset-heavy, squarely our ICP — reports adoption nearly tripling, 38,000 tickets deflected, 34% faster resolution.

They can copy us

HARC AMS is ten months old with no product org. Win with a feature and they replicate it — free, inside the platform.

03 Bear case · where they’re structurally weak

They sell software that describes governance. Someone still has to run it.

Pricing = ill will

Steep costs that climb, 5–10% escalators, and metered “assists” CFOs cannot forecast. Their own cost can’t be governed.

“Any system” = lock-in

Now Assist depends on platform-resident data. Every incentive pulls workloads onto Now. Neutrality from a platform vendor is always discounted — ours isn’t.

Stops at the IT layer

Governs tickets, workflows, apps. No MES, SCADA, historian, plant edge. No IEC 62443, NERC CIP, FDA Part 11.

Ops, not software

72% have agents in production; ~21% have mature governance. The gap persisted while dashboards proliferated.

Half is roadmap

Key May enhancements sit in Innovation Lab, GA expected Aug 2026. The shipped product is thinner than the keynote.

07 Reconciliation

Both cases are true — at different layers.

Control-plane layer
Bull case wins
Distribution, bundling, and the Microsoft alliance make ServiceNow the default governance dashboard. Stop pretending otherwise.
Operations & OT layer
Bear case wins
Nobody has closed the gap between owning a dashboard and operating governed agents against physical, regulated infrastructure — and their pricing prevents them from closing it cheaply.
ServiceNow is our most dangerous competitor only if we insist on selling what they sell. We sell what they structurally can’t: fixed-price, regulator-grade agent governance operations — below and beside their tower.
08 Three moves for Q3 · Move 1

Launch “Governed Agent Operations” as a standalone priced offer.

ModelFixed $/agent-under-management/month — out of the services bundle
First packNERC CIP + IEC 62443 for energy & manufacturing, operated from HARC
Targets10 accounts — 5 existing HARC clients, 5 cold CISO/CRO buying centers
What has to be true

Buyers pay for governance ops as a separate line from delivery, and vertical compliance packs are hard enough that ServiceNow won’t replicate them for a market this narrow.

What proves it wrong

Fewer than 3 signed paid pilots at ≥$150K implied ARR within 90 days — or every pilot signed only by our existing delivery sponsor.

09 Three moves for Q3 · Move 2

Integrate with the tower instead of fighting it.

Certify HARC AMS as a feeder into AI Control Tower via Action Fabric/MCP and into Agent 365 via Entra Agent ID. Their tower sees the enterprise; we enforce at the plant. Channel conflict becomes co-sell.

What has to be true

ServiceNow tolerates coexistence because OT coverage helps them win regulated accounts, and joint clients see the plant-layer gap as real.

What proves it wrong

They ship credible OT/edge governance themselves, restrict our integration, or cut us out of two-plus joint pursuits. Then the partnership lane is closed — retreat to Move 1 standalone.

10 Three moves for Q3 · Move 3

Run the CFO wedge.

Build a TCO comparison: our fixed per-agent price against assist-metering with retry-loop exposure, escalators, and 75% hidden first-year costs. Aim at accounts with ServiceNow renewals inside 12 months. A marketing campaign that feeds pipeline into Move 1 — not a product bet.

What has to be true

Pricing resentment changes buying behavior rather than just generating grumbling — and the CFO has veto power over agentic spend.

What proves it wrong

Win/loss data shows price never appears in the top three decision criteria — or ServiceNow moves decisively to fixed pricing (watch whether assists get capped).

11 One discipline note

All three moves share a single kill switch.

If Move 1’s 90-day sell-through fails and Move 3 generates no qualified pipeline —

the market is telling us governance is a feature, not a product. We fold AMS back into delivery permanently and monetize this space by implementing ServiceNow’s tower better than Accenture does. That is a fine business too.

What we should not do is spend four more quarters half-productizing.
