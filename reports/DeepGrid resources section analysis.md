# Fix the evidence before the library

DeepGrid v11's Resources section has a lot of engineering depth, but it is not yet safe to put in front of an investor. Three pages state, as unqualified fact, figures that the site's own claim register withholds or contradicts. The whitepaper's reading edition still shows "₹2.88 Crore" of "Contracted Revenue", including "Indian Army MCEME ₹1.01 Cr". It carries the banner "The source edition, unchanged", while `app/claims.ts:177-181` withholds that same ₹1.01 Cr as misdescribed. The first "Start here" video credits a road demo to "the DG-A100", a part that exists only as an "On request" title with no file behind it. The "Three-Factory" document and card say "validated on test silicon" and "Status: DONE — Physical working silicon fabricated & validated", while the evidence route says "No DeepGrid part has measured silicon yet." Beyond integrity, the section works far better for the secondary audience (equipment-maker engineers) than for the primary one. On mobile, /resources is about 21,900 px of engineering material with **one** in-body "Request a discussion" link at the very bottom. There are about 12 "Ask DeepGrid" buttons and no data-room path. Peers show the fix. TI and Infineon stamp every pre-silicon document with its status. Infineon lists gated titles with version and date. Axelera and Tenstorrent open with a "start here" by role. DeepGrid already publishes far more than Indian peers such as Mindgrove, InCore and Netrasemi, so its problem is labelling and consistency, not volume. The ranked fix list below starts with about ten claim and evidence edits, most of them one-line changes in `app/documents-data.ts`, `app/resources-data.ts` and two Markdown files. These should ship before any further investor outreach.

Method and scope: the research notes rendered the live pages at 1440 px and 390 px with Playwright on 2026-11-02. They checked every load against the repo `shekerkamma/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11` at HEAD 4103fd7 (27 Oct 2026). All file references below are repo-relative `path:line` in that repo. View counts come from the Return YouTube Dislike cache because YouTube's bot wall blocked direct reads. Treat them as within-channel signals only.

## Four subsections, one engineering library, no investor lane

The section has four surfaces, and all four were built for an engineer evaluating parts. **/resources**, the landing page, has the H1 "Technical documents, decks and films." Its lead is "Start with the portfolio for product scope, the evidence register for maturity, or the detailed documents for integration", but none of the three starting points it names is a link ([Resources](https://shekerkamma.github.io/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11/resources/); copy from `app/executive-routes.json:92-97`). Below that comes a 9-card document hub with filters and search, then a 5-package deck-and-film player (61 slides, about 29 minutes), a DG32-LITE diagram block, collapsed external references, 7 legacy jump buttons, a 4-image concept gallery, a "next decision" box and related links. The main element holds **25 download links, 14 reader links, 9 cards, 22 images and exactly 1 in-content contact link**. At 390 px the decks and films begin about **10,615 px** down a **21,906 px** page ([Resources](https://shekerkamma.github.io/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11/resources/)).

**/resources/docs** ("Technical specifications") says "Nine product specifications open to read; 21 more documents on request." It renders 9 cards in raw registry order, then 7 direct PDF downloads in four groups, then an accordion of 21 mailto-only titles ([Docs](https://shekerkamma.github.io/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11/resources/docs); `app/resources-data.ts:87-113`, `app/company-page.tsx:280-339`). Five of the 7 downloads duplicate a card under a different title. The page shows **8 unique PDFs and 3 concept write-ups with no PDF (D100, SDV, Three-Factory), 11 distinct items in total**. Its hero ("Access technical documentation, datasheets, whitepapers, and development resources") and its CTA ("Contact Support") read like a support desk (`app/company-pages.ts:181-189`).

**/resources/videos** ("Product walkthroughs") holds **51 items**: 10 self-hosted part films of 1:04–1:30, each narrated and with a transcript, plus 41 YouTube videos, 28 of them long-form and 13 Shorts. Only **21 of the 51 have transcripts**, and every ADAS, D-Mirror and D-Drive demo except D-Base is silent ([Videos](https://shekerkamma.github.io/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11/resources/videos); `public/transcripts/index.json`). The H1 is "Ten narrated part films, plus the DG32 fault path in 1 min 26 s." The "Start here" row below it then leads with four legacy picks: a 19:12 silent road demo, a 0:22 mirror clip, a 10:55 Follow-Me video with no narration, and a 2023-era T-Hub interview (`app/resources-data.ts:66`). The page has no contact CTA. Its exits are "Subscribe to the DeepGrid Semi channel", "Narrated decks & films" and "Documentation".

**/resources/read** is the in-site Markdown reader at `?doc=<path>`. It builds an "In this document" table of contents from H2 headings only (`app/doc-reader.tsx:58-59`). Every document gets one fixed banner. No document has version, date or status metadata, and every browser tab is titled "Source document · DeepGrid Semi" (`app/resources/read/layout.tsx:3`). The site's source of record, the 11,010-word SKU Blueprint, renders with **0 TOC entries** because it uses 143 H3 headings and no H2 ([Blueprint reader](https://shekerkamma.github.io/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11/resources/read?doc=docs%2Fdeepgrid-sku-blueprint-oct2026)). The owner's brief is unambiguous: "Investors and strategic partners are the primary audience… The primary action is a discussion or data-room enquiry" (`PRODUCT.md:8`). The routing ignores it. The /resources and /read route stories both end in `"next": "/resources/docs"`, not `/contact` (`app/executive-routes.json:92-97,110-115`). The only reading-order aid on /resources is a three-step box, and its step 01 sends readers to the **superseded** Technical Annex v3 compendium (`app/route-journey.tsx:35`) rather than the October 2026 Blueprint that the docs page calls "the current portfolio".

## Three claims an investor would catch in the first hour

### The withheld MCEME order is republished as "source edition, unchanged"

The whitepaper card opens a reading edition (`public/downloads/docs/deepgrid-mature-silicon-architecture.md`) that says "**Live Pre-ASIC Contracted Revenue: ₹2.88 Crore (Indian Army MCEME ₹1.01 Cr, Infinis Agritech ₹1.25 Cr, Axitech Solar ₹0.62 Cr)**" at line 16. It repeats "Indian Army MCEME (₹1.01 Cr Contracted)" in a table row at line 106 ([whitepaper reader](https://shekerkamma.github.io/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11/resources/read?doc=docs%2Fdeepgrid-mature-silicon-architecture)). The site's own claim register withholds that figure because "₹23.01L delivered, ₹78.39L is L1 (lowest bidder, not an award), and it sits in Deepgrid Datacentre Pvt Ltd rather than Deepgrid Semi" (`app/claims.ts:177-181`). The October Blueprint says the opposite of the whitepaper: "this book claims no purchase orders or letters of intent." The site did write a warning, "Its orders figure includes a contract this site withholds as misdescribed; do not cite it" (`app/documents-data.ts:172,296`). But the cards render only the first sentence of each summary (`d.summary.split(/(?<=\.)\s/)[0]`, `app/company-pages.ts:185`), so the live whitepaper card reads only "The foundational 71-page master whitepaper establishing India's mature-node semiconductor doctrine." The string "supersed" does not appear anywhere on the whitepaper or Three-Factory reader pages ([Docs](https://shekerkamma.github.io/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11/resources/docs)). This is the most serious defect in the section. Three things combine: a revenue figure the company has itself disowned, a banner that vouches for it, and the warning hidden by a string split. The same `.md` file is also the cited source for 3 of the 15 register claims (`node-130`, `loop-198`, `asil-d`; `app/claims.ts:115-140`), so it cannot simply be deleted. It has to be corrected in place.

The other whitepaper-versus-Blueprint conflicts compound the problem. The whitepaper says "Ten chips" while the Blueprint says "Eleven chips… plus the Track B drone SoC". The whitepaper says "200 MHz"; the Blueprint says "130 nm chip at 100 MHz"; the DG32 cards say "50 MHz". The whitepaper describes one "Angel / seed round · ₹10 Cr", while the Blueprint describes a ₹10 Cr tranche 1 plus a "₹40 Cr in May 2027" tranche 2. The D100 and Three-Factory reading editions still say "Fixed 200 MHz" (`deepgrid-three-factory-architecture.md:52`, `deepgrid-d100-architecture.md:22,35`), and nothing on the page reconciles these figures.

### The "Start here" road demo credits a part that does not exist

Item 1 of "Start here" on /resources/videos is "Live ADAS Demo on Hyderabad City Roads" (19:12). Its card text says "Thermal vision and real-time perception running live on Hitech City roads **with the DG-A100**" (`app/resources-data.ts:18,66`). DG-A100 appears in the codebase only as a request-only datasheet title (`app/resources-data.ts:96-97`). The video's own YouTube description says the system is "Ad0", combining third-party models "CLRerNet" and "YOLOv26n", with "performance on embedded hardware", and it is tagged "FPGA AI". It never mentions DG-A100 ([YouTube qBHUWiiw4Po](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qBHUWiiw4Po)). An investor would read the site's version as DeepGrid silicon running on a road, and DeepGrid has no silicon. "Start here #4" adds a second dated problem. In the T-Hub interview the founder says "we are planning to tape out using TSMC in 2025. our chipset will be out" and describes a 28-core design that is not DG32 ([YouTube AOaSVtRbvRk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AOaSVtRbvRk); `public/transcripts/AOaSVtRbvRk.json`). As of November 2026 that is an unmet forward claim featured as an introduction. The Softmax video is labelled "the silicon behind the DG-R100 accelerator" (`app/resources-data.ts:26`), while the docs page calls DG-R100 a "Radar SoC", which is a second fictional SKU used inconsistently.

### "Real tape-out", "validated on test silicon" and "Status: DONE"

Four separate wordings imply that fabricated silicon exists:

- The Anatomy video's site title is "13 words from a real tape-out", and its narration says "explained on a real 2.9 by 4.5 mm silicon die… The die is the piece of silicon we get back" (`public/transcripts/b4VFlC5GDzY.json`).
- The Three-Factory card highlights "Zero-NRE open-source toolchain (OpenLane/Magic/KLayout) **validated on test silicon**" and "Proof of concept for India's ₹10 Cr sovereign chip initiative" (`app/documents-data.ts:293-301`).
- Its reading edition shows "Status: DONE — Physical working silicon fabricated & validated" for the sky130 tier (`public/downloads/docs/deepgrid-three-factory-architecture.md:30`) ([reader](https://shekerkamma.github.io/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11/resources/read?doc=docs%2Fdeepgrid-three-factory-architecture)).
- The DG32 die tour opens "our full 130 nanometer riskv microcontroller die running live" (`public/transcripts/Bw34e4sx144.json`).

All four contradict "No DeepGrid part has measured silicon yet; first silicon is the December 2026 shuttle" (`app/executive-routes.json:83`). Further down the same risk ladder, the card summaries assert "Formal proof that high-accuracy industrial condition monitoring… do not require heavy transformer acceleration" (`app/documents-data.ts:48`), "worst-case execution time guaranteed untouched" (`:143`), "creates statutory moats" (`:172`) and "Sanction-proof multi-foundry PDKs". Every card's list is headed "Key architectural proofs" (`app/documents-hub.tsx:205`). The D100 reading edition says "DGCA Type Certification: Compliant with Directorate General of Civil Aviation standard civil airworthiness regulations" (`deepgrid-d100-architecture.md:117`). The site's own style rule bans exactly this register: `docs/site-story.md:85-88` lists "Authoritative", "Verified" and "sovereign supply chain security" as language that "asserts trust instead of showing the source."

The banner is a broader problem. "The source edition, unchanged" (`app/doc-reader.tsx:131`) is true for **only 1 of 9** reading editions, the Blueprint, whose `.md` declares "Text and tables are taken from the PDF unchanged" with `[pdf p.N]` markers. The other 8 are 7–13 KB derived write-ups. The whitepaper edition is 10.7 KB standing in for a 71-page PDF. D100, SDV and Three-Factory state "Generated Via: `/architecture-to-everything`", and DShot says "drafted with Claude" ([Docs](https://shekerkamma.github.io/deepgrid-dr-silicon-v11/resources/docs)). Some cards also disagree with their own reading editions. The D100 card says "one 28 nm chip… Quad RV64", while the edition describes a multi-die SiP with "Dual DGridRiscV Flight Cores… at 200 MHz". The Three-Factory card names "SkyWater, SCL Mohali, and Dholera 28nm fabs", while the edition names SkyWater, IHP Leibniz and SCL Mohali. Publication hygiene adds two more risks. Publicly downloadable files carry "CONFIDENTIAL" and "Confidential Silicon Diligence Canon" labels, and the whitepaper edition prints the managing director's mobile number in its header (`deepgrid-mature-silicon-architecture.md:4`).

Some of this is done well and should be kept as the model. The part films say "Animations illustrate; they are not simulations. Pre-silicon; every figure is a design target" and link to the evidence register (`app/video-library.tsx:197-201`). The latch-up narration says "This chip has never been fabricated", and Wrapalign admits "Chip Foundry caught it. Our 26 green sign-off modules had not" ([YouTube VYNbuuYrXSg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VYNbuuYrXSg)). The SDV card says "ISO 26262 ASIL-D (target, not certified)". The investor checklists the benchmark cites reward this candour. They name "honest identification of remaining technical challenges" as a green flag and "a 2-level or larger gap between claimed TRL and observed TRL" as a red one ([AESTECHNO](https://www.aestechno.com/en/hardware-technical-due-diligence-investor-guide/)), and they tell founders to "start with a plain summary of what works today and what is still unproven" ([startupdatarooms.org](https://startupdatarooms.org/use-cases/deep-tech-startups)).

## Peers stamp status per asset and gate safety material

The credible peers split public material from gated material and put a maturity stamp on every asset. TI prints "PRODUCT PREVIEW information concerns products in the formative or design phase… specifications are design goals" on its datasheets ([TI product-stage statements](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/sced011a/sced011a.pdf)). Infineon labels an app note as "based on… pre-silicon data, including chip simulation… The values will be updated based on post-silicon measurement data". It also publishes an "On request" table showing title, description and version/date for each gated document ([Infineon TC4xx AN](https://documentation.infineon.com/aurixtc4xx/docs/znw1761050437240)). That is the same idea as DeepGrid's "21 more documents on request", but with the metadata that makes it believable. NXP shows "Rev 12, Nov 2025" and file size for each document and keeps the safety manual and FMEDA in a SafeAssure NDA group ([NXP S32K3](https://www.nxp.com/products/S32K3)). Infineon's safety package (safety case, safety manual, FMEDA tool, analysis summary) is "made available to customers under non-disclosure agreement" ([Infineon FuSa](https://documentation.infineon.com/aurixtc3xx/docs/owq1745576218449)). Ambiq shows the date and size of every file ([Ambiq Resources](https://ambiq.com/resources/)). Axelera and Tenstorrent lead with role- and task-based "start here" entry points ([Axelera SDK](https://docs.axelera.ai/sdk/); [Tenstorrent Docs](https://docs.tenstorrent.com/)). The Indian startups publish almost nothing. Mindgrove has product cards and a contact form, and InCore offers form-gated brochures ([Mindgrove](https://www.mindgrovetech.in/); [InCore](https://incoresemi.com/)). DeepGrid is therefore ahead of its local peer set on depth and behind incumbents on labelling. The benchmark found **no fabless startup with a public evidence register**, so `app/claims.ts`, applied consistently, is a real differentiator. Today it is undercut by the documents sitting next to it.

Three benchmark findings bear directly on the fix list. First, peers keep strategy and policy papers apart from engineering specs. DeepGrid's docs page mixes "Three-Factory Sovereign Silicon" and a defence whitepaper with RTL specs, and it reuses copy written for deepgridsemi.com's AI-accelerator products: "Detailed specifications and technical documentation for all our AI accelerators" (`app/resources-data.ts:91`) and "Go inside our AI accelerators" (`:69`). Second, a pre-silicon safety MCU should publish a safety-concept overview and a list of planned safety work products, and hold back FMEDA numbers. DeepGrid publishes no safety-concept document at all, even though its lockstep part is "designed against" ASIL-D (`app/claims.ts:133-139`), and the 21 request-only titles do not include one. Third, the "on request" list names four SoCs that are not in the Blueprint's 11 SKUs: "DG-A100 ADAS SoC, DG-R100 Radar SoC, DG-S100 SDV Controller, DG-T100 Transformer NPU" (`app/resources-data.ts:96`). This is the same legacy taxonomy that produced the DG-A100 credit. Investor data-room guidance describes a tiered structure: Level 1 public (deck, architecture, papers, team), Level 2 after a CDA (model, IP summary, BOM), and Level 3 confirmatory ([Cashpian](https://www.cashpian.com/what-should-be-in-a-deep-tech-fundraising-data-room)). DeepGrid's mailto list could become a named Level-2 index at almost no cost.

On video, Wistia's 2026 data puts play rate on Resources pages at **6%**, against 24% on home pages and 15% on product pages. It also finds 1–3-minute educational videos the most engaging format ([Wistia 2026](https://downloads.ctfassets.net/j7pfe8y48ry3/5tOf2aVNOvk9CValTgj83h/f577dc1aebb439a6904c253b9f80c23c/Wistia-2026-State-Of-Video-Report.pdf)). The 1:04–1:30 part films are the right length and belong on the SKU pages as well. The 11:21 "Anatomy" film needs chapter timestamps, as Ambiq provides on its 28:06 CTO walkthrough ([Ambiq Video Library](https://ambiq.com/video-library/)). Peers label demo hardware informally ("running locally on Axelera silicon"), so a structured "Runs on" field per DeepGrid video would go beyond peer practice exactly where DeepGrid needs it most.

## What the podcast lens adds, and where it stops

KJ Rainey's two rules are to rank what a reader should see by importance to their goal and to title for the viewer with a short hook. The first rule applies directly to the documents. For an investor, the Blueprint should come first: it covers the current portfolio, unit economics, the ₹10 Cr / ₹40 Cr tranches, and its admission of "no purchase orders". Next come /evidence, then the QFN-64 datasheet as the single proof-point part, then the whitepaper (with a visible "superseded in part; orders figure withheld" stamp), and last the D100, SDV and Three-Factory concept architectures, labelled as concepts. For an engineer, the order is the DG32-LITE datasheet, the QFN-64 datasheet, the DG32-LITE architecture guide, the 2DOM datasheet and block definition, the DShot spec (draft 1), and then "Thirty use cases" as the nearest thing to an application note. The ranking exposes one gap. `dg32-lite-architecture-guide.md` is the source for **6 of the 15** register claims (`app/claims.ts:34-87`), yet it is not one of the 9 cards.

The outlier lens says the channel's winners are physical motion. "D-Drive 360° Movement" has 1,213 views (23× the median of 52), "D-Base Alpha v1" has 759, and the thermal "See Through Glare" clip has 442. These are robotics and mirror clips, not silicon. The best silicon titles are "Anatomy… 13 words" (166) and Softmax (158) ([RYD](https://returnyoutubedislikeapi.com/votes?videoId=qG8XNHRXZWk)). All 32 measured videos total only 4,261 views, so the outliers are a packaging signal, not traction to quote. On the site the lens has to defer to the investor purpose. The robots are popular, but they are an unlabelled third "evidence universe" alongside DG32 layout and the earlier FPGA/third-party ADAS work, so they belong in an "Earlier platform work" section, not in "Start here". The title rule is still useful because number hooks drawn from real design data are both clickable and verifiable. On a 0–4 score (viewer-centred, hook, 50 characters or fewer, claim-safe), the T-Hub and Retrofit titles score 0 and Lockstep scores 1. Proposed replacements, all under 50 characters, are "13 chip words, explained on our DG32 layout" (43), "The power bug our 26 sign-off checks missed" (43), "One missing via could have killed this chip" (43), "A 3.9 mm safety MCU, layer by layer" (35) and "19 minutes of Hyderabad traffic, thermal ADAS" (45). The last one only works with the card text changed to "Third-party models on prototype hardware; not DeepGrid silicon."

## Conclusion

The cost of fixing this section is low, which makes the current state riskier, not safer. The ten integrity fixes are text edits, and the site already contains the right answers: the claim register, the part-film caveat, the latch-up narration and the Blueprint's "no purchase orders". The defects come from contradicting material that was never brought in line, and from a string split that hid a warning the team had already written. An investor's diligence associate who opens the whitepaper card first, then checks /evidence, will find the company contradicting itself on revenue. That damage is far larger than the benefit of any number of datasheets.

The structural point goes beyond these pages. The evidence register is the one asset no peer has, but it only earns trust if every document, card and video points back to it with a status stamp. Once each asset carries `edition`, `status` and "Runs on" fields, the investor lane, the Level-2 data-room index and the Rainey-style ranked reading paths become simple rendering choices on top of trustworthy metadata. A few things remain unverified: what hardware actually ran the road, mirror and D-Drive demos (only the founder can confirm), whether the 21 request-only documents exist, and how the contact page handles `intent=investor`. Close those three questions before the Tier 2 data-room promise goes live.
