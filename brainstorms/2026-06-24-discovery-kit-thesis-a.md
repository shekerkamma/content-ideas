## GOLDEN RULE (non-negotiable)
**Every prospect conversation drives toward a PAID validation pilot.** The only acceptable substitute for a paid pilot is GTM *discovering* pre-existing, real, budgeted demand (a bank already trying to solve this, already spending). **No free pilots. No "interesting, keep me posted." Paid or a hard no.**

## BAU CORRECTION (supersedes earlier "first agents" choice)
Do NOT lead with commodity agents (generic support chatbots #1/#5, generic doc-extraction #19). They are BAU, low-stakes, vendor-benchmarked → mild validation pain. **Lead with novel-autonomy, high-stakes, hard-to-validate FS agents:**
1. **Customer-facing advisory / action-taking agents** — suitability, UDAAP, fair-lending, conduct risk (an agent that recommends products or moves money).
2. **Agentic decisioning** — credit / underwriting / collections → adverse-action notices + fair-lending (ECOA/Reg B) exposure.
3. **AML / fraud investigation agents** (#24, KEEP/high-moat) — SAR quality, alert disposition; model-risk-sensitive.
4. **Agentic auto-remediation in regulated infra** (#23) — operational, change-control, audit risk.
Value prop: "We validate the autonomous, consequential agents your model-risk team can't — the ones your examiner is already asking about." Objective-credibility demo can still use a deterministic slice (e.g., adverse-action notice accuracy, AML alert-disposition precision) but inside a high-stakes context.

## MRM / CRO DISCOVERY SCRIPT (15–20 min)
Goal = find one bank with a CURRENTLY BLOCKED autonomous agent + willingness to pay. Listen for behavior/money, not interest.

1. **Status quo:** "When a line of business hands you an AI *agent* — one that takes actions or makes decisions, not just a chatbot — to put into production, what do you do today to validate it? Walk me through the last time."
2. **Pain + cost:** "Where does that process break or stall? What does a blocked or delayed agent go-live cost you — in time, exam risk, or business friction?"
3. **Acute trigger:** "Is there an agent your business wants to ship *right now* that you can't get comfortable signing off on? Which one? Why is it stuck?"
4. **Current spend (demand proof):** "Who validates these today — your team, a consultancy, the vendor? What are you spending on model validation / external review annually?"
5. **Examiner pressure:** "Have examiners asked about AI/agent model risk in your last exam? What did they want to see?"
6. **The paid ask (always close here):** "If we did an independent, examiner-ready validation of one of your autonomous agents — a paid pilot — would that be worth a [$X] engagement to you this quarter? Who else would need to say yes?"

### Demand-evidence rubric (score every call)
- 🟢 REAL DEMAND: names a blocked agent + current validation spend + says yes (or "send me the SOW") to a paid pilot.
- 🟡 SIGNAL: acknowledges the pain + exam pressure but no budget/urgency yet → nurture, not a pilot.
- 🔴 NO DEMAND: "interesting," "keep me posted," no blocked agent, no spend → disqualify. Do not mistake politeness for demand.

## THE PAID OFFER — "Agent Validation Teardown" (one-pager)
- **What:** a fixed-fee, 2–3 week independent validation of ONE autonomous agent → an examiner-ready validation dossier (conceptual soundness, outcome testing vs our FS Agent Validation Benchmark v0 + adversarial red-team, monitoring thresholds, control attestations) + a readout to MRM/CRO.
- **Why paid:** the fee IS the demand test. A bank that won't pay a modest fee for examiner-ready assurance does not have the pain.
- **Price anchor:** position against what they already pay for external model validation (5-figure per-model engagements) — price the teardown as a familiar line item, not a new category.
- **Deliverable they keep:** the dossier they can put in front of their examiner. Their win is a faster, safer go-live with cover.
- **Our win:** revenue + the first failure-corpus data + a reference logo + benchmark validation.
