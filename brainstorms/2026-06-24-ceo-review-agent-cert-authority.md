## Strategic Review (adapted sections)

### S1 — Strategy / "Architecture"
The wedge (MRM/SR 11-7) is the strongest part — it converts a new budget ask into an existing mandated line. KEEP. The weak joint is sequencing: the doc lists services→platform→benchmark but doesn't gate spend on evidence. FIX: insert hard kill-gates (below).

### S2 — Business Risk & Threat Model
- **Existential:** no demand (P-A), no insider credibility (P-B). → both must be resolved in the next 60 days or pivot.
- **Competitive:** Big 4 + Patronus (compliance-first evals) move in. Defense = independence + examiner acceptance + FS depth + benchmark authorship. Thin if we have no insider.
- **Regulatory:** self-attestation blessed → demand collapses. Monitor OCC/Fed AI guidance.
- **Channel conflict:** if we both certify AND partner with Big 4, manage the "auditor independence" line carefully.

### S3 — GTM / Unit-Economics Edge Cases
- Services margins early; subscription annuity later. Risk: stuck in low-margin bespoke consulting (the "services trap"). Guardrail: every engagement must templatize into the product or we stop.
- Long bank sales cycles vs startup runway. Mitigate: super-regional/mid-tier first; design-partner discounts for data rights to the failure corpus.

### S4 — Observability (how we'll KNOW it's working)
Leading indicators: # MRM discovery calls → # naming a *currently blocked* agent → # signing a paid pilot → benchmark reviewed by an ex-examiner. Lagging: pilot→subscription conversion, NDR, examiner citations.

### S5 — Deployment / Rollout Sequencing
Phase 0 (now, 60d): discovery + 1 paid teardown + recruit MRM insider. Phase 1: 1 paid validation pilot + benchmark v0. Phase 2: productize + 3-5 banks + publish benchmark. NO platform engineering before Phase 1 is paid.

### S6 — Reversibility / Future-Fit
Highly reversible at Phase 0 (just our time). The ratchet thesis (regulation tightens post-incident) is sound and specific. Future-fit: STRONG if the insider + examiner acceptance land; WEAK without them.
