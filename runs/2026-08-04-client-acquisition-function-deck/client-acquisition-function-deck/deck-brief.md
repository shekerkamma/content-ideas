# Deck brief

## Audience

Founder-led professional-services firms, agency principals, revenue leaders, and operators who have useful prompts but no dependable acquisition operating system.

## Decision

Approve a staged 90-day program that validates one acquisition task at a time, establishes the business context layer, and pilots a governed four-role acquisition team without autonomous external sending.

## Narrative promise

Client acquisition becomes dependable when the organization compounds evidence, context, artifacts, and review gates in sequence; “always on” is an earned operating state, not a software feature.

## Voice and tone

Operator-grade, direct, commercially literate, and skeptical of automation theatre. Use action titles, clear boundaries, qualitative depth, and measurable promotion gates. Avoid inflated AI claims.

## Anti-references

Generic AI dashboards, prompt collections presented as strategy, decorative technology imagery, shallow listicles, repetitive card walls, and claims of autonomy without observed run evidence.

## Evidence and editability

The supplied DeepGrid investor briefing is a visual-system reference, not factual evidence for acquisition claims. All diagrams, scorecards, states, tables, and roadmaps remain PowerPoint-native and editable. The 10-level acquisition framework and operating guardrails come from the user-supplied client-acquisition playbook and its codified skill.

## Success criteria

- At least 50 substantive slides; delivered plan contains 75.
- The slide-title sequence tells the full argument.
- Every level has a purpose, artifact, gate, and failure mode.
- The operating model names states, owners, handoffs, controls, metrics, and stop conditions.
- Structural validation, contract validation, design lint, contact-sheet review, and real-render QA are recorded.
