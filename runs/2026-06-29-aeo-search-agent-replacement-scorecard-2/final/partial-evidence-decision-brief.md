## What The Evidence Supports

### 1. The strongest wedge is not “AI replaces SaaS.” It is “AI changes which SaaS layers remain defensible.”

The captures repeatedly separate durable systems from replaceable workflow layers. Core platforms survive when they hold records, permissions, governance, integrations, audit trails, or proprietary context. Add-ons, assistant modules, drafting layers, and repetitive execution work are more exposed.

Use this as the main positioning frame:

> AI agents do not replace every SaaS product. They compress the workflow/interface layer first, while systems of record and governed data layers survive longer.

### 2. Human sign-off is a first-class scoring dimension.

Claude and Gemini both describe AI as useful for drafting, triage, summarization, first-line support, research assistance, and structured workflow acceleration. They also preserve human accountability for regulated, ambiguous, customer-sensitive, legal, or high-risk decisions.

The scorecard should not ask only “can an agent do this?” It should ask:

- What can be automated end-to-end?
- What needs human review?
- What creates legal, brand, financial, or customer risk?
- What escalation path is required?

### 3. Pilotability is a conversion lever.

The evidence repeatedly points to bounded tests: containment rate, resolution rate, cycle time, escalation quality, conversion rate, draft quality, and historical-example replay.

This suggests the scorecard should output a pilot plan, not just a replacement score. A buyer needs to know how to test the thesis before using it in procurement or renewal conversations.

### 4. Reddit points to buyer skepticism, not just demand.

The Reddit evidence is useful because it exposes objections:

- AI output looks polished but may be low substance.
- Buyers distrust vague targeting, black-box automation, and weak reporting.
- Operators care about workload reduction, ROI, precision, and implementation risk.
- In SaaS org design, people already discuss role compression and account ownership consolidation.

This buyer language should shape prompts and content. The scorecard should directly address skepticism instead of sounding like another generic agent-replacement thesis.

## What Not To Claim Yet

Do not claim:

- “AI engines recommend our scorecard.”
- “We have full AEO evidence readiness.”
- “ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google AI Mode all agree.”
- “This is statistically validated.”

Current evidence supports a directional thesis and workflow design, not a final market-proof claim.

## Recommended Next Asset

Build the scorecard around five scoring dimensions:

1. Workflow determinism
2. Data moat and system-of-record dependency
3. Human sign-off boundary
4. Pilotability and measurable test design
5. Renewal or seat-compression leverage

Each scorecard output should produce:

- replacement risk level
- what layer is exposed
- what layer survives
- required human checkpoint
- pilot test
- procurement/renewal talking point

## Content Strategy From This Evidence

Create pages that answer non-branded buyer questions:

- “Where are AI agents realistically useful in customer support workflows?”
- “Can AI agents replace doc summarization and drafting software?”
- “When does AI reduce SaaS seats versus just assist users?”
- “How should teams pilot an AI agent before replacing a workflow tool?”
- “Which SaaS workflows are protected by proprietary data and governance?”

Each page should include:

- concrete workflow examples
- named software categories
- human-review boundaries
- metrics for testing
- comparison language
- caveats and failure modes
