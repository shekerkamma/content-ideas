# Legal Research & Drafting: Traditional Tools vs. AI Agents

## Current Standard Workflows

**Research Phase:**
- **Westlaw/LexisNexis** - dominant legal databases with citators (KeyCite/Shepard's)
- **Fastcase/Casetext** - lower-cost alternatives
- **Google Scholar** - free case law access
- **Practice guides & treatises** - secondary sources
- Manual citation checking and validation

**Drafting Phase:**
- **Microsoft Word** - still the standard (with track changes, comments)
- **Document automation tools** - HotDocs, Contract Express for templates
- **Matter management systems** - Clio, MyCase for small firms
- **DMS (Document Management)** - iManage, NetDocuments for large firms
- Manual copy-paste from prior work, form files, precedent banks

## Where AI Agents Are Actually Useful

**✅ Research assistance:**
- Summarizing case law and identifying relevant precedents
- Finding statutory/regulatory language across jurisdictions
- Initial "what does the law say about X" scoping queries
- Extracting holdings and key facts from long opinions

**✅ First-draft generation:**
- Routine motions, discovery responses, demand letters
- Contract clause drafting from templates
- Translating business requirements into legal language
- Reformatting/restructuring existing text

**✅ Review & analysis:**
- Spotting inconsistencies across document sets
- Comparing contract versions (going beyond redlines)
- Checking defined terms usage
- Due diligence document review and categorization

## Where AI Agents Fall Short

**❌ Citation reliability:**
- Hallucinating cases remains a critical problem
- Can't fully replace Shepardizing/KeyCiting
- Risk of citing overturned or distinguishable cases

**❌ Jurisdiction-specific nuance:**
- Misses local rules, standing orders, judge preferences
- Struggles with circuit splits and evolving standards
- Can conflate similar-but-distinct doctrines

**❌ Strategic judgment:**
- Can't assess which arguments will persuade *this* judge
- Doesn't weigh litigation risk vs. settlement posture
- Misses client-specific business context

**❌ Complex novel issues:**
- First-impression questions requiring analogical reasoning
- Interdisciplinary problems (tax + IP + corporate structure)
- Predicting how courts will apply law to new technology

## Realistic Hybrid Workflows (2024)

Many forward-looking teams are using:

1. **AI for initial research** → validate with traditional databases
2. **AI-generated first drafts** → senior attorney heavy editing
3. **AI document review** → attorney judgment on flagged items
4. **Human strategy/AI execution** - lawyer outlines argument, AI drafts sections

**Tools gaining traction:**
- Harvey AI, CoCounsel (Thomson Reuters), Lexis+ AI
- vLex Vincent, Casetext CoCounsel
- General LLMs (Claude, ChatGPT) with careful verification

## The Real Workflow Bottlenecks AI Hasn't Solved

- Client communication and expectation management
- Internal firm knowledge management (finding *our* prior work)
- Court filing system quirks (many still require physical/fax)
- Negotiation and adversarial strategy
- Ethical judgment calls

The pattern I see: **AI is becoming a capable junior associate for mechanical tasks, but still needs senior supervision for anything that matters.**
