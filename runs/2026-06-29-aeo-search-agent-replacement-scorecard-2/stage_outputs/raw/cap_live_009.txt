# Current Reality of Doc Workflows & AI Agents

## Traditional Software Stack

**Most teams still use:**
- **Word processors** (Word, Google Docs) + manual summarization
- **Legal/enterprise**: iManage, NetDocuments with built-in summaries
- **Knowledge management**: Confluence, Notion with manual TL;DRs
- **Contract tools**: Ironclad, DocuSign CLM with template libraries

**Workflows are surprisingly manual:**
- Someone reads the doc and writes a summary email/memo
- Teams maintain template libraries and copy-paste-edit
- Junior staff spend hours distilling partner/exec decks
- Review cycles involve lots of "can you shorten this?" feedback

## Where AI Agents Are Actually Useful Now

**✅ High-value, working today:**

1. **First-draft summarization** - Save 30-60 min per long doc
   - Works: Board decks, research papers, legal briefs, RFPs
   - Limitation: Still need human review for accuracy/tone

2. **Bulk document processing** - Analyze 50 contracts in minutes vs. days
   - Works: Due diligence, compliance reviews, extracting key terms
   - Limitation: Can't replace final human judgment on risk

3. **Format conversion/restructuring** - Turn meeting notes into memos
   - Works: Meeting → action items, report → exec summary
   - Limitation: Loses context that wasn't explicitly written

4. **Template-based drafting** - Generate standard docs 10x faster
   - Works: NDAs, standard responses, status reports
   - Limitation: Anything requiring creativity/negotiation still needs humans

## Where AI Agents Fall Short

**❌ Overpromised, underdelivering:**

1. **Complex synthesis requiring judgment**
   - Merging conflicting stakeholder input
   - Strategic recommendations based on docs
   - "Read these 5 contracts and tell me our negotiation position"

2. **Nuanced tone/persuasion**
   - Investor pitches
   - Diplomatic communications
   - Anything where subtext matters

3. **Maintaining context across long projects**
   - Remembering decisions from 3 months ago
   - Consistent voice across 50-page reports
   - Understanding unstated organizational politics

4. **Full autonomous workflows**
   - "Analyze this, draft response, and send" - too risky
   - Requires human checkpoints at each stage

## Realistic Adoption Pattern

**What actually works:**

```
Human defines scope → AI generates draft → Human edits → AI reformats → Human approves
```

**Not:** AI does everything autonomously

**Successful teams use AI as:**
- A **research assistant** (find relevant sections across docs)
- A **first-drafter** (80% draft in 5 min vs. 60 min blank page)
- A **formatting helper** (convert bullet points to prose)

**They don't use it for:**
- Final decision-making
- Anything customer/court-facing without heavy review
- Situations where error cost is high

## The Integration Gap

Most AI is still **bolted on** rather than integrated:
- Copy-paste into ChatGPT/Claude (security risk)
- Browser extensions that feel clunky
- IT departments blocking everything due to data concerns

**The winners will be:**
- Tools embedded in existing workflows (Office 365 Copilot approach)
- API integrations with document management systems
- Companies solving the compliance/security piece properly

---

**TL;DR:** AI agents are legitimately useful for **speeding up drafting** and **processing volume**, but the "autonomous AI paralegal/analyst" vision is oversold. The realistic use case is "make good people 2-3x faster" not "replace people."
