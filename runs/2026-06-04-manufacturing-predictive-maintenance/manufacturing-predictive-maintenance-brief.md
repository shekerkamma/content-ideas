# Executive Brief: Manufacturing Predictive Maintenance

## The Signal
Hyundai and MakinaRocks said on April 23, 2026 that their predictive robotics maintenance system is being expanded across major production hubs and is expected to reach about 1,400 robots by the end of 2026, with failures predicted up to five days in advance at over 90% accuracy.

## The Opportunity
This use case attacks a pain category large enough to matter. NIST cites downtime at 8.3% of planned production time and $245B in discrete manufacturing cost. Deloitte reports smart-manufacturing adopters are already seeing 10% to 20% output gains, while predictive maintenance benchmarks point to lower downtime, fewer defects, and better labor productivity. The practical wedge is not “AI for all maintenance.” It is a plant-by-plant rollout on high-utilization robotic cells and bottleneck equipment where downtime is visible and SAP PM is already the action system.

## Who Is Winning
Hyundai and MakinaRocks are the strongest proof point in this source set because they show scaled deployment, not just a pilot. SAP is also positioned well because Asset Performance Management, Digital Manufacturing, and Field Service Management already connect maintenance insight to enterprise workflows. On the orchestration side, verified OpenHands capabilities now matter as an implementation accelerator: the SDK, CLI/headless mode, MCP support, and self-hosted enterprise deployment model make it plausible to run domain-specific maintenance agents without inventing an agent runtime from scratch. The winning architecture is therefore SAP-native on the workflow side and OpenHands-compatible on the orchestration side, not a standalone model lab.

## The Risk
This market is real, but it is easy to oversell. Deloitte lists poorly defined business cases, increasing technical complexity, and inadequate change management as common implementation blockers. In practice, many plants have the sensor data but not the cross-functional operating model to trust alerts, schedule interventions, and close the feedback loop.

## Framework Fit
The vertical scores GO at 25/35. It has strong intelligence density, clear ROI, and acceptable outsourcing readiness. The cap is incumbent presence and execution risk, not lack of demand.

## Verdict
Go, with a narrow entry wedge. Lead with one asset family, integrate directly into SAP PM, and sell the program as “reduced downtime through faster maintenance action” rather than as a generic AI platform. Use OpenHands internally to orchestrate the agent workflow, MCP connectors, and repeatable delivery playbook.
