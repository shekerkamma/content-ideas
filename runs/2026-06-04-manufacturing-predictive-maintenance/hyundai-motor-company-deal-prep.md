# Presales Deal Prep: Hyundai Motor Company

## Prospect snapshot
- Hyundai Motor Company is already public proof that predictive maintenance in automotive manufacturing can scale beyond a lab pilot.
- MakinaRocks says Hyundai is expanding predictive robotics AI across major production hubs and EV lines, with deployment expected across about 1,400 robots by the end of 2026.
- The account context is not “should Hyundai do predictive maintenance?” It is “where can Hyundai extend, operationalize, or standardize the capability further?”

## 3 key facts
1. Hyundai is using predictive robotics maintenance in production environments today.
2. The published motion is global scale-out, not one-plant experimentation.
3. The likely executive narrative is Software-Defined Factory, hyperproductivity, and zero-defect quality.

## Positioning angle
Position the offer as the layer that accelerates the next wave of rollout: faster onboarding of additional asset classes, tighter SAP PM integration, clearer technician workflows, reusable cross-plant deployment patterns, and an OpenHands-based orchestration layer that standardizes how agents, tools, and runbooks are packaged internally.

## Likely opening line
"You already proved predictive maintenance on robotics. The next value unlock is reducing rollout friction across more asset families and more plants without creating another analytics silo."

## Top objections and responses
- "We already have a partner for this."
  - Response: The wedge is not replacing the core model vendor. It is accelerating integration, workflow design, and cross-plant packaging around SAP PM and maintenance operations, using OpenHands as the reusable execution layer behind the scenes.
- "We are standardizing internally."
  - Response: Good. The best fit is a scoped accelerator team that codifies reusable deployment patterns your internal organization can keep using.
- "Cybersecurity and plant disruption risk are too high."
  - Response: Start edge-first, read-only where possible, and limit the pilot to one asset family with controlled work-order automation.

## Meeting prep
- Ask which asset classes beyond robotics have the clearest downtime pain.
- Ask whether SAP PM, APM, or another maintenance workflow is the system of action today.
- Ask how technician feedback is captured and whether false positives are blocking trust.
- Ask how they decide a pilot is ready for global replication.

## Recommended next step
Propose a plant-specific discovery sprint that maps one new asset family from telemetry to SAP PM work-order action and quantifies the operational gap between current robotics rollout and broader manufacturing standardization. Structure the sprint to leave behind reusable OpenHands skills, agent definitions, and integration patterns for the next plant.
