# Competitive Threat Analysis - Phase 1

## Threat 1: Incumbent Platform Expansion

Microsoft, Salesforce, ServiceNow, SAP, Oracle, Adobe, Workday, Atlassian, Box, Zoom, Snowflake, and Databricks can embed AI into existing procurement paths.

Implication: startups must win on workflow depth, speed, or measurable ROI, not generic AI access.

## Threat 2: Foundation Model Commoditization

As model performance converges for many enterprise workflows, value moves toward data access, workflow integration, governance, and distribution.

Implication: model wrappers are fragile unless they own workflow context or proprietary data.

## Threat 3: Open-Source Substitution

Open and open-weight model ecosystems pressure proprietary pricing and enable enterprises to self-host or use lower-cost providers.

Implication: vendors need operational reliability, support, and governance beyond model access.

## Threat 4: Cloud And Data Platform Bundling

Cloud/data platforms can pull AI workloads into governed infrastructure where enterprise data already lives.

Implication: standalone vendors need strong integrations or a buyer pain that data clouds do not solve natively.

## Threat 5: Vertical SaaS Embedding AI

Existing vertical SaaS vendors can add AI features inside workflows they already own.

Implication: vertical AI startups need a wedge that is materially better than an incumbent feature add-on.

## Threat 6: Consulting And Services Capture

Large consultancies can capture AI strategy budgets and shape vendor selection before product vendors enter the room.

Implication: a repeatable operator system must show faster proof and more concrete artifacts than traditional discovery.

## Threat 7: Governance And Security Blockers

Agents with tool access create data leakage, compliance, hallucination, and auditability concerns.

Implication: governance and security are not optional add-ons; they are deployment gates.
