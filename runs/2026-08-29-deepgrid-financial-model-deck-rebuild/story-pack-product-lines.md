# Story pack — DeepGrid Semi product-line pitch deck

## 1. BLUF
One 28 nm chip turns a legal compliance obligation into a thirteen-line, ₹1,128 Cr
product portfolio — and this round buys that chip.

## 2. Audience decision
Pre-Series A investors should conclude: **fund the silicon.** The portfolio is not a
strategy to be believed, it is a consequence of one tapeout. Judge the round on whether
the chip lands, not on whether thirteen separate businesses succeed.

## 3. Tension
India's ADAS obligation is live now — driver-drowsiness, blind-spot and lane-departure
from April and October 2026. Every N2/N3 truck built or retrofitted has to comply. Today
that compliance is met with imported compute at $3,000–8,000 a module. If no Indian part
exists, the mandate is satisfied with foreign silicon at foreign margins, and the
platform position closes as fitments standardise. **The window is the fitment cycle, not
the market forecast.**

## 4. Argument arc
1. **Context** — The mandate sets a demand floor: 1.0 M trucks a year, by law.
2. **Tension** — That demand is served today by imported compute at $3,000–8,000.
3. **The move** — One 28 nm SoC designed for it, at $3.88 a die at volume.
4. **Proof: portfolio** — The same chip becomes thirteen products. The portfolio is a
   consequence of the silicon, not thirteen separate bets.
5. **Proof: concentration** — Two mandate-adjacent lines carry 64%. Deliberate, not accidental.
6. **Proof: margin ladder** — Systems carry volume at 89%; silicon carries profit at 94%;
   sensors are carried for capability at 50%.
7. **Reality check** — Under 2% of every sized pool, prices flat for six years, and one
   line already paid. A floor, not a stretch.
8. **Action** — Release capital against silicon, certification and paying customers.

## 6. Evidence map
- **Direct evidence:** unit and price build; segment margins; demand pools with named
  third-party sources; GSR 184(E) dates; ₹1 Cr defence revenue received; $3.88 die cost
  derived from die size, yield and wafer price.
- **Fair synthesis:** tiering into core / silicon / adjacent / sensors; the claim that the
  portfolio is a consequence of one tapeout; margin-ladder reading.
- **Interpretation (soften or move to notes):** the fitment-cycle window; competitive
  displacement of imported modules; T100 licence economics (no sized pool exists).

## 7. Content cuts
- The 13-slide one-per-product catalogue. It implies equal weight where two lines carry 64%.
- Per-product "who buys it" as a standalone block on minor lines — fold into the column.
- The $3,000–8,000 vs $3.88 comparison as a headline multiple (774×). It compares a bare
  die to a complete module; present it as a like-for-like caveat, not a claim.

## 8. Rebuild instructions
- Open on the decision, not the catalogue: exec summary at slide 2.
- Beats 1–3 (mandate → imported silicon → our chip) must precede any product content.
  Without them the portfolio reads as a list.
- Only AD2 and AD0 get full slides. Everything else is grouped by tier.
- Every tier slide states its ₹ Cr and share in the subtitle so the arc keeps its shape.
- Close on the gate list, matching the walkthrough deck's diligence language.
