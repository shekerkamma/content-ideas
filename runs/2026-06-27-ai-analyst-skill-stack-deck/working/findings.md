# Findings

## Headline Finding

The strongest positioning is not "we have blueprints." It is: "we have a 10-skill operator stack that replaces the expensive traditional dev-shop path from diagnostic to post-launch iteration, and we have applied it across 52 reviewed agentic opportunities."

## Finding 1: The 10 Skills Form a Full Engagement Lifecycle

The stack covers the whole gamut:

1. Validate whether the problem is real.
2. Cut the scope to a 30-day MVP.
3. Design the technical architecture.
4. Decide build vs buy.
5. Gate ROI and breakeven viability.
6. Tear down competitors and incumbent weakness.
7. Define acceptance criteria and edge-case QA.
8. Design data architecture and analytics from day 1.
9. Sequence deployment, smoke tests, and rollback.
10. Control post-launch iteration and pivot decisions.

This is materially broader than implementation blueprints. Implementation architecture is Skill #3 and part of Skill #7/#8/#9, not the full offer.

## Finding 2: The Value Proposition Is Compression, Not Cheapness

The source prompts repeatedly name expensive traditional phases:

- $10K-$15K diagnostic phase.
- $15K-$25K scope document.
- $20K-$40K architecture phase.
- $30K of build-vs-buy SOW analysis.
- $15K market scan.
- $20K-$35K shipping definition / QA phase.

The market message should be that the system compresses these phases into an AI-native operator workflow. Avoid saying "ours is $100K"; say the old way often burns that level of budget before the MVP proves anything.

## Finding 3: The Human Operator Gate Is the Differentiator

Each skill includes "Where Claude breaks." That section is not a footnote; it is the trust mechanism.

The operator adds judgment where raw AI is weak:

- Representative user evidence.
- Real buying signal vs polite interest.
- Regulatory and distribution constraints.
- Founder scope discipline.
- Integration-time realism.
- RLS and data access safety.
- SaaS pricing creep and lock-in.
- Positioning patterns, not feature lists.
- Cross-feature edge cases.
- Source-of-truth failures.
- Production webhook and env-var failures.
- Post-launch feature discipline.

This makes the offer credible: it is not "AI writes documents." It is "AI accelerates the workflow while an operator applies the gates that prevent false confidence."

## Finding 4: The 52 Opportunities Prove Surface Area

The current portfolio has 52 implementation-reviewed opportunity files. They should appear as a proof set, not as the deck's main structure.

Recommended framing:

- "The method scales across 52 reviewed opportunities."
- "Each opportunity can be passed through the same 10 gates."
- "The portfolio demonstrates repeatability across support, HR, compliance, security, legal, finance, healthcare, retail, logistics, marketing, and operations."

## Finding 5: Market Mapping Is Not Yet the Same as Complete Teardown Proof

There are teardown and market-map artifacts in the prior run, but the validated audit currently proves implementation depth. A final client-facing claim that every use case has fully verified incumbent pricing should be backed by per-use-case teardown source files.

Recommended wording:

"Competitor teardown is a required Skill #6 gate. The portfolio has implementation-reviewed blueprints; incumbent-specific pricing and market teardowns should be source-attached before final external publication."

## Correct Deck Thesis

Traditional dev-shop discovery asks founders to spend heavily before learning whether the MVP should exist. This 10-skill operator stack turns that journey into a gated, repeatable 30-day system: validate the problem, cut the scope, design the architecture, make build-vs-buy decisions, pressure-test ROI, map competitors, define QA, wire data, deploy safely, and iterate without founder chaos. The 52 reviewed opportunities prove that this is not a one-off idea list; it is a reusable capability engine.
