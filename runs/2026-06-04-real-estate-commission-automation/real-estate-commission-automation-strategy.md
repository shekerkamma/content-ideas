# Full Strategy: Brokerage Commission & Deal Payout Automation

## Strategic thesis

Commission and payout automation is worth pursuing when the offer is framed around back-office accuracy, payout speed, and exception reduction rather than generic AI assistance.

## Why the wedge works

- Commission logic is repetitive but complex enough to create manual pain.
- Finance and ops teams are used to tooling and outsourced support.
- Errors, delays, and exception queues create a visible ROI story.

## Ideal entry point

1. Ingest transaction and commission-plan data.
2. Validate split logic, caps, fees, and payout readiness.
3. Route approvals, reconciliation tasks, and payout packets.
4. Expand into broader transaction, accounting, and agent-management workflows.

## Solution-stack recommendation

- Back-office and accounting systems remain the systems of action.
- OpenHands orchestrates payout validation, exception handling, and workflow skills.
- Docusign or equivalent transaction-document workflows provide agreement and packet state.
- Reporting focuses on payout cycle time, exception rates, approval latency, and transaction-to-cash throughput.

## Delivery model

- Phase 1: payout validation and exception workflow
- Phase 2: accounting handoffs and CDA/document packet automation
- Phase 3: broader brokerage back-office workflow packaging

## Risks

- Bad payout logic erodes trust quickly
- System fragmentation creates integration friction
- Finance teams may resist opaque AI behavior
- Brokerage plan exceptions may sprawl without policy discipline

## Controls

- Keep approval and release authority with finance or ops
- Encode explicit plan rules and exception policies
- Start with one broker or one ops team before portfolio-wide rollout
- Keep an auditable trace of each workflow step

## Recommended account angle

Target brokerages or networks with enough transaction volume that payout accuracy and speed visibly matter. A RE/MAX-style network or a mid-market brokerage platform team is the cleanest entry angle.
