## 1. The Business Problem

### What Is Happening Today

**Client Intake Backlog:**
A small litigation or transactional firm receives 20–40 new inquiries per month —
web forms, referral calls, bar association referrals. Each inquiry requires a conflict
check, a preliminary matter type classification, a retainer estimate, and a response
to the potential client within 24–48 hours. Firms that respond within 5 minutes convert
78% more leads than firms that respond in 5 hours. Most firms respond in 24 hours
because the intake process is manual. **They are losing 30–40% of their convertible leads
to the first firm that picks up the phone or responds to the form.**

**Unbilled Time:**
Studies consistently show attorneys capture only 60–75% of billable time — not because
they worked less, but because contemporaneous time entry is painful. Lawyers who
reconstruct time at the end of the day under-bill by 20–30%. At a $300/hour billing
rate, a solo attorney leaving 1.5 hours unbilled per day loses **$112,500 per year.**
An AI system that prompts time capture in real-time — connected to your calendar,
your document opens, your email exchanges — recovers a significant portion of that.

**Routine Document Assembly:**
Engagement letters, retainer agreements, NDAs, basic contract reviews, and standard
corporate filings follow templates that are 90–95% identical across matters. The
attorney spends 45–90 minutes customizing each from scratch, or pays a paralegal
to do it for 30 minutes. Neither is a good use of legal capacity.

## 2. The Proposed Solution

We deploy an AI administrative team that handles the non-legal work: intake, time
prompting, and document assembly — all staged for attorney review before anything
leaves the firm.

Every workflow is scoped to administrative tasks only. Nothing in this system drafts
legal advice, constructs legal arguments, or represents the firm's legal position.
Attorneys review and approve all output.

### What Gets Built (Initial Engagement)

**Workflow 1 — Client Intake Automation**
When a new inquiry arrives (web form, email, or intake call transcript), your AI team
runs an automated conflict check against your case management system, classifies the
matter type, sends the prospective client a same-day acknowledgment with next-step
instructions, and creates a new matter record in Clio or PracticePanther with all
intake data pre-populated. Attorney receives a clean intake summary, not a raw form.

*Converts: 30–40% more leads through faster first response*
*Saves: 2–4 hours per week on intake data entry*

**Workflow 2 — Billable Time Prompting + Capture**
Your AI team monitors your calendar for completed meetings, document opens, and
email exchanges, and sends a time-entry prompt within 30 minutes of each activity:
"You had a 45-min call with [Client] re [Matter] at 2pm — enter time?" One click
confirms. The draft entry is submitted to Clio for attorney review.
No time goes to Clio without attorney confirmation — it is a prompt, not auto-posting.

*Recovers: 0.5–1.5 hours of unbilled time per day · At $300/hr: $37,500–$112,500/yr*

**Workflow 3 (Month 2 expansion) — Document Assembly for Routine Matters**
Engagement letters, retainer agreements, basic NDAs, and standard corporate document
requests are assembled from your firm's templates using matter data already in your
CMS. Attorney receives a pre-populated draft for review and signature — not a blank
template to fill in. Customization time drops from 45–90 minutes to 10–15 minutes.

*Saves: 45–75 minutes per routine document · Scales without increasing paralegal cost*

## 4. Compliance Architecture

Legal administrative automation requires careful scoping.

| Boundary | How We Maintain It |
|---|---|
| No legal advice | All AI output is clearly marked as draft/administrative; attorneys review all client communications |
| No UPL risk | AI team performs intake classification and data entry only; matter strategy remains attorney work |
| Conflict check | Automated system flags potential conflicts; attorney makes the conflict determination |
| ABA 1.1 (Competence) | Attorneys review all AI-generated documents before use; no auto-filing or auto-send |
| Client data privacy | All client data in attorney-controlled infrastructure; no client data shared with third-party AI APIs |
| Attorney-client privilege | All matter data stays in firm's own CMS; no privilege waiver risk from internal automation |
