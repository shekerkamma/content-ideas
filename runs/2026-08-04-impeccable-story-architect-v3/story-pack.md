# Story pack — Impeccable 4.0 client adoption decision

## 1. BLUF

Impeccable should be piloted as a governed decision system for design—not adopted as an autonomous
taste engine—because the walkthrough demonstrates stronger choice architecture and faster local
comparison, but provides no measured evidence of productivity, usability, or business impact.

## 2. Audience decision

Authorize a matched-task pilot on one real landing-page workflow. Do not authorize broad rollout.
The pilot must hold the brief, evidence, references, model budget, and acceptance criteria constant;
name a human direction owner; and use blind quality, accessibility, performance, provenance, and
cost review before expansion.

## 3. Tension

AI can produce a plausible first page quickly, but plausibility creates anchoring. If taste enters
only after a generic composition hardens, teams spend their most expensive cycles undoing layout,
assets, and hierarchy. Impeccable answers that problem by introducing more choices—but more choice
can create a second failure: indecision, rerolls, and operator-dependent quality. The management
question is therefore not “Does the demo look better?” It is “Does this decision architecture
reduce wrong-direction cycles without creating new quality and governance failures?”

## 4. Argument arc

1. **Answer first:** pilot the decision architecture; do not scale the promise.
2. **Expose the hidden cost:** generic first drafts move directional debate downstream.
3. **Explain the mechanism:** Worlds separates visual-grammar selection from execution choices.
4. **Show the boundary:** Live Mode accelerates local comparison only after direction is sound.
5. **Name the escalation:** references and terminal work reset a wrong macro direction.
6. **Install the operating model:** humans own truth, direction, trade-offs, and release; the system expands, executes, detects, and records.
7. **Bound the evidence:** the walkthrough reaches structured convergence, not measured optimization.
8. **Make the decision executable:** readiness gates, a matched-task pilot, control ledger, and expansion criteria.

## 6. Evidence map

### Direct evidence from the walkthrough

- Live Mode supports element selection, one-to-four variants, tuning, and acceptance.
- Worlds presents materially different macro directions, followed by variants within a selected direction.
- The user can reroll or steer the direction.
- The demonstration uses a Pathfinder landing page and a “Find the why” component comparison.
- Accepted choices feed into the design record and terminal workflow.
- References are used to drive a large macro-direction change.
- The presenter explicitly says Impeccable has a lower floor and higher customization ceiling than narrow one-style skills.

### Fair synthesis

- The workflow separates divergent exploration from convergent refinement.
- More choice creates value only when commitment gates prevent drift.
- Live Mode is best suited to bounded local decisions after macro direction is accepted.
- References function as an escalation/reset mechanism when the visual grammar is wrong.
- The design record can reduce reinvention but can also preserve stale constraints.

### Interpretation or recommendation

- Generic defaults increase downstream rework.
- The optimal operating model allocates human judgment to four irreversible moments.
- The walkthrough reaches maturity Level 2 and elements of Level 3.
- A matched-task pilot is the correct adoption path.
- Proposed pilot thresholds are decision scaffolding, not validated benchmarks.

Interpretations remain visibly qualified or in the supporting evidence line; they are never presented
as measured results.

## 7. Content cuts

- Cut GitHub-star counts: popularity does not prove client value.
- Cut a command-by-command tour: it explains features but weakens the adoption argument.
- Cut most of the 17 recovered frames: a screenshot sequence would substitute interface chronology for synthesis.
- Cut claims that Impeccable “improves quality,” “saves time,” or “improves conversion”: the source supplies no comparative data.
- Cut external-gallery examples from the client deck: they informed composition only and are not evidence or licensed client assets.
- Cut internal workflow terms, raw timestamps, source paths, and production labels from visible slide copy.
- Cut monetary opportunity sizing: the transcript contains no task volume, labor cost, or attributable lift.
