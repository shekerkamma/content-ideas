# AI Analyst findings

## Decision question

Should a client adopt Impeccable 4.0 as part of its AI-assisted design operating model—and where
should human judgment, terminal generation, Live Mode, references, and review controls sit?

## Headline finding

Impeccable's primary value is not better generation. It reallocates scarce human judgment to four
high-leverage moments: framing the truth, committing to a macro direction, comparing local
alternatives, and challenging the result before release.

## Mechanism

The workflow creates structured divergence followed by visual convergence:

1. Ground the brief in surface, stack, evidence, and references.
2. Explore materially different Worlds.
3. Commit to a macro direction.
4. Compare expressions within the chosen World.
5. Build through the terminal.
6. Use Live Mode for bounded component decisions.
7. Record accepted decisions in the design system.
8. Use detection and fresh-context review as challenge controls.
9. Reset through references and terminal work when the direction—not the detail—is wrong.

## Evidence standard

- `OD`: observed demonstration in the supplied walkthrough.
- `PA`: presenter assertion, not independently validated.
- `AI`: analyst inference derived from the demonstrated workflow.

The source is one favorable walkthrough. It supports workflow analysis, not measured claims of
speed, design quality, usability, conversion, or production readiness.

## Central trade-off

Generality raises the creative ceiling but lowers the operating floor. More options add value only
when the organization supplies decision ownership, stopping rules, evidence discipline, and
independent QA.

## Adoption recommendation

Pilot on one bounded landing-page workflow. Keep real evidence and approved references fixed,
stratify operators by design experience, blind the quality review, retain conventional technical
QA, and expand only if wrong-direction cycles fall without quality or governance regressions.

## Metrics to validate

- Time to first acceptable direction and production-ready result
- Major direction resets and post-approval rework
- Worlds/variants reviewed before commitment
- Blind scores for hierarchy, coherence, distinctiveness, brand fit, and usability
- Accessibility, responsive, browser, and performance defects
- Unsupported claims and untracked reference exceptions
- Reviewer precision/recall and design-record drift
- Tool/model cost per accepted result
