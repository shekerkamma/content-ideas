3 India: Customer and use cases

6 India: Go-to-market and channel

Sequence: retrofit → government → OEM line-fit → Phase-3 software.

First (now): 5t retrofit. The Shravan-T1-funded 2-truck, 90-day Smart Mirror demo program
   PLAN   is the proof asset; the 100-unit DGS001 pipeline (§4) bridges cash. Every contract
carries a data-rights clause (edge-case harvest, model-improvement license) — the data moat
is real only if contractually secured.
Second: government/PSU/defence (§3 Customer 2); the GeM record             CONTRACTED     opens
doors.

Third — the mandate channel, reframed as upside, not a committed date. OEM/Tier-1
design-in is relationship-gated: comparable regulation-created hardware markets (EU
tachograph, eCall) show mandate volume normally flows to whichever supplier already has
OEM relationships and homologation experience, not the cheapest new entrant — those
incumbents typically absorbed aftermarket share within ~18 months once line-fit normalized
  EXTERNAL REFERENCE   . This plan therefore pursues OEM/Tier-1 engagement opportunistically
from month one (a) AIS-162/188 certification (T2-funded, §9)       PLAN   , (b) retrofit field-
hours and Indian-road data as validation, (c) the T2 milestone clock (§9) — but does not treat
a signed design-in as a required, dated event the way §8's revenue trajectory implicitly
assumes. The pitch leads with capability, not price: owning perception + compute + model on
Jiyu's <100 ms ASIL-D EHB lets us certify a single photon-to-brake latency number no
mix-and-match integration can produce — but that pitch needs an actual OEM contact, which
this plan does not yet have (§10 risk 10).

Phase-3 expansion: yard-owning retrofit logos convert to driverless pilots under the same
single-signer structure — acceptance criteria as moves/day and incidents — then multi-year
PVPM contracts; a reusable site playbook cheapens each successive deployment.

Channel economics, promoted from footnote to primary near-term engine. Government/
defence (§3 Customer 2) and insurer-co-funded retrofit are the two revenue lines that do not
depend on the AIS-certification clock, the ASIC-tapeout clock, or an OEM relationship
DeepGrid does not yet have — they should therefore carry FY27–FY28 revenue, not the
OEM channel above. Insurer co-funding works like fleet UBI/telematics programs elsewhere:
an insurer subsidizes part of the kit cost against a premium discount priced off the ADAS
telemetry stream, cutting effective retrofit CAC    ASSUMPTION   and giving DeepGrid a second
paying customer for the same install. Brogen and Jiyu need a perception supplier and don't
build one — no channel conflict.
   Fast case vs. slow case. The plan as financially modeled (§8) is a fast case: certification,
   tapeout and first OEM engagement all land inside 12–18 months. Comparable mandate-
   driven hardware markets have historically taken 3–5 years to move from mandate
   announcement to stable incumbent share       EXTERNAL REFERENCE    . In the slow case, FY27–
   FY29 revenue rests almost entirely on government/defence and insurer-co-funded retrofit
   (this section) while certification and OEM engagement continue in the background with no
   committed date — the fast case is upside, not the base the company should be funded
   against.

Moat, in order of durability:

 1. Structural price floor: owned silicon at <$3–4/bare die (₹1,750 packaged, §5) makes the
   ₹2.3L price ours to hold; importers cannot follow down without destroying their own
   margin stack.

 2. Certification + sovereignty: claimed only-indigenous AIS-validated chipset, AIS-004
   native, 15 patents filed; foreign silicon excluded from government/defence fleets (§2).
   Certification is a path, not held — the killer risk, §10.

 3. Data moat that compounds — narrower than it sounds. D-Drive trained on Indian
   roads (cattle, autos, unmarked lanes, fog, no HD maps); the retrofit flywheel — sell the
   mirror, own the miles, upgrade in place — widens the gap with every kit (contractual per
   §6). Honest caveat: Continental and Bosch both already manufacture in India with
   decades of India-specific automotive-electronics experience         EXTERNAL REFERENCE    —

   holds for a pure importer with no India footprint; the defensible claim is narrower: they
   have not yet built an India-trained perception model at this price point, not that they
   structurally cannot.

 4. The photon-to-brake artifact (§6): one certifiable end-to-end latency number no bolt-

Why the $1M comes in one tranche, not two

The earlier design split the strategic entry into two $500K tranches, with the second released
on milestones. We have changed that: the full $1M is taken in a single allotment at ₹112
Cr, and the milestones move from gating the money to gating the equity. The reason is not
preference — it is that the two-tranche structure is very likely unexecutable by the time the
second tranche falls due.
