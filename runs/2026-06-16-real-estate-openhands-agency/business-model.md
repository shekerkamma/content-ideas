**External (buyer-facing):**
> "Your AI Engineering Team for Real Estate Brokerages"

**Tagline:**
> "The software your brokerage needs. Without the developer you can't afford."

**Never say:** OpenHands, subagents, MCP servers, AI agents, LLM, prompts.
**Always say:** your AI team, the system, the bot, we built, it handles.

---

## Why This Position Is Defensible

Real estate agents already outsource: transaction coordinators, marketing VAs,
ISAs. They're already paying someone to do this work. You're not selling them a
new concept — you're selling a better delivery mechanism for work they're already buying.

The moat compounds over time:
- Month 1: TC Bot saves $2,000/mo — they won't cancel
- Month 3: Lead scoring is inside their CRM — they can't cancel without migrating
- Month 6: CMA generator, listing pipeline, commission dashboard all running —
  you are now their entire operations layer. They have no vendor relationship to
  replace you with.

## The ROI Conversation (30 seconds)

For the TC Bot conversation:

> "You're paying $300-$500 per transaction to your TC. If you close 6 deals a month,
> that's $1,800-$3,000 a month. I charge $2,500. So you break even at 5 transactions
> and come out ahead on everything above that. Plus you stop chasing people yourself."

For the Lead Scoring conversation:

> "If lead scoring finds you one extra closed deal a year — at $10,000 average commission
> — that's $833/month in commission value for $2,500/month. Two extra deals and you're
> 2.5× ahead. Most agents I've talked to say they lose at least 3-4 deals a year to
> bad follow-up."

For the listing description system:

> "How long does it take you to write a listing description? 45 minutes? At your hourly
> value that's $200-$400 of your time per listing. If you have 3 listings a month,
> that's $600-$1,200 you're spending writing the same thing in slightly different words.
> This does it in 3 minutes."

## Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "How is this different from Dotloop/BoomTown?" | Dotloop is a document storage tool. BoomTown is a CRM. This builds custom software for your specific workflow — not a subscription to someone else's template. It does what you need, not what they built for the median agent. |
| "How is this different from Zapier?" | Zapier connects existing apps. This builds software that didn't exist — reads your purchase agreements, thinks, makes decisions, and acts. It's not a trigger-action tool; it's an engineering team. |
| "What if the AI makes a mistake?" | You supervise. Every workflow has an exception trigger — if something looks wrong, it stops and texts you. You only touch the 5% that needs judgment. The 95% that's always the same runs without you. |
| "Can I cancel if it doesn't work?" | 30 days notice, no penalty. I'm not interested in clients who aren't seeing value. If it doesn't work in 30 days, I'll tell you before you do. |
| "Why can't I just use ChatGPT for this?" | ChatGPT is a conversation. This reads your actual contracts, connects to your actual calendar, sends emails from your actual email address, and tracks deals in your actual CRM. It's the difference between asking someone how to do it and having someone do it. |
| "Is it going to replace my TC?" | Your TC handles the exceptions — the difficult conversations, the negotiations, the judgment calls. This handles the tracking, the chasing, the daily status emails, the document collection. Your TC stops doing admin and starts doing what they're actually good at. |
