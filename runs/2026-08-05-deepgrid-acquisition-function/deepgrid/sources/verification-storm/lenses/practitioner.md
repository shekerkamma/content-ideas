# Practitioner lens — DeepGrid client acquisition

Operator view: someone who has sold hardware into Indian fleets and government buyers. Items
marked *(judgment)* are experience, not sourced data.

## 1. Track A is sellable — but not as the thing the deck says it sells

Slides 10 / 26 / 37-Condition-1 treat the ₹1 Cr as one open question: grant or commercial sale?
Wrong question. BP-1A §3 names the contracts — GeM GEMC-511687794620175 "Robot Training
Assistant" ₹23.01L delivered, and GeM RA GEM/2026/R/672471 "Robotics Assistant" ₹78.39L
**L1, under execution**. BP-1A §9 (Entity hygiene) adds that it sits in **Deepgrid Datacentre Pvt
Ltd**, that **IP on the delivered contract is MCEME-owned (Clause 27)**, and that **"none of it is
trucking/ADAS revenue."**

Condition 1 therefore passes and proves nothing. A robotics training-aid delivery to an army training
establishment is not a reference for a UGV, border or port-AGV compute node; slide 10's "Technical
fit (ships today) 5/5" is not evidenced by it. Also: **L1 is not an award** — booking ₹78.39L as
CONTRACTED is aggressive *(judgment)* — and **the GeM record is on the wrong entity**, since seller
registration, past-performance and turnover attach to a PAN/GST entity. Deepgrid Semi bids from zero.

FPGA hardware and the missing AIS cert are non-issues here; AIS is a road-homologation regime,
irrelevant to a UGV. The blocker is that no programme exists — slide 15 lists the champion as **"not
yet identified."** A UGV/border programme runs service-HQ QR → RFI → RFP → seasonal field trials
→ maintainability evaluation → CNC, not GeM: a multi-year motion *(judgment; no sourced duration)*.
Slide 26's "second signed defence contract by Month 6" is realistically reachable only by selling more
Datacentre-style services work, which does not build the chip story. Port AGV is worse — the named
terminals (sl.26) run incumbent-owned automation stacks, and slide 19 marks the SI channel "NOT
YET — no named partner today."

## 2. The Track B kill criterion is fast on the calendar, naive in definition

20 interviews across 13 weeks is *slow* — slide 20 puts 10 calls in weeks 1–4, 11–20 in weeks 5–9.
A founder does 20 fleet conversations in three weeks. That is research cadence, not selling.

The naive part is conversion. Slide 20 Phase 1 says "**No commercial ask yet**"; Phase 2 expects a
paid-pilot term sheet from the same cohort by week 9. You cannot run 27-question discovery with no
ask and convert that person six weeks later — a paid fleet pilot needs a truck they can ride, a price, a
written SLA, a warranty path, and a budget window opening on a fiscal or post-monsoon cycle
*(judgment)*. It is also gameable: one friendly logo clears it, and what gets piloted is the FPGA kit at
~₹1.27L COGS / 45% GM (BP-1A §5), so a two-truck pilot is likely contribution-negative after install,
calibration, travel and alert tuning. **Winning this test can cost more than failing it.**

Rewrite it: one paid pilot with **money taken before install** and a **published day-30 uptime
number**. BP-1A §5 already names day-30 uptime as the renewal driver and cites the AIS-140 tamper
precedent — the only test that matters, and the deck omits it.

## 3. What breaks in the channel

**The documents contradict each other on insurers.** Slide 13 calls insurers anti-ICP and "never a
direct customer"; slide 19 marks the channel "NEVER (SALES)". BP-1A §6 promotes insurer-co-funded
retrofit to a **primary near-term revenue engine**. A commercial lead cannot tell whether to make the
call. Operationally, co-funding needs a filed, actuarially-supported product and claims evidence that
does not exist — the slowest channel, not the fastest *(judgment)*.

**NBFCs do not decide fitment.** BP-1A §6 says they "decide what gets fitted on a financed truck."
They decide LTV and what is fundable inside the loan. Adding ₹2.3L against an asset whose resale
value does not rise by ₹2.3L is an LTV problem — and residual value is exactly what DeepGrid cannot
evidence until used-truck valuers recognise the kit.

**Recruiting a dealer for a ₹2.3L kit** takes visible margin (realistically 15–25% of ASP = ₹35–58k,
eating a third to half of the ₹1.04L gross profit per kit in BP-1A §5 and breaking the 45% FPGA-phase
GM), demo stock DeepGrid funds, a calibration jig and trained technicians per bay, an RMA commitment
and 30–60 day credit *(judgment)* — all of which BP-1A concedes is "not yet modelled." The structural
break: a dealer service counter can *fulfil* a ₹2.3L capex decision but cannot *originate* it. The plan
conflates installation capacity with demand generation.

## 4. The middle of the volume range is the unfunded zone

400–600 kits/year (BP-1A §5) is 35–50 installs a month: two to four bays, six to ten technicians,
three to five anchor accounts, two cities, ~₹9–14 Cr revenue. **Direct sales covers that — no channel
needed.** The channel exists only for the FY32 story.

The middle — 1,000–1,500 kits/month against the 4,000/month FY32 requirement — has no plan
behind it: 30–50 bays, 150–250 certified technicians, a spares depot, a defined RMA loop. Working
capital alone at ~₹1.27L COGS × 1,200 kits/month on 60–90 day channel terms runs to tens of crores,
against a ₹45 Cr round already fully allocated (BP-1A §9) — which is why BP-1A calls receivables and
kit inventory "the largest unmodeled cash consumers." Fundable at 400–600/year, unfundable in the
middle, no bridge between them.

## 5. First 90 days, done differently

1. Week 1: both GeM contracts and the MCEME Clause 27 IP schedule in front of the investor. Replace
   Condition 1 with: which entity, which product line, who owns the IP, is there a repeat order. Then
   fix the entity — Semi becomes the GeM contracting party, or it bids from zero and says so.
2. 30 fleet conversations in six weeks, not 20 in nine, with a **price sheet from call five**. Measure
   quote requests, not interest. Price the stripped compliance SKU inside those calls.
3. Two paid installs within weekly driving distance. Money before install. Instrument tamper from day
   one. Publish day-30 uptime.
4. Recruit no dealers, NBFCs or insurers this quarter — you cannot recruit a channel with zero installed
   base and no warranty apparatus. Recruit one 3PL or plant operator with 50–150 trucks who buys
   *and* installs on their own premises: a channel of one that also secures data rights.
5. Freeze the port-AGV lane until an SI or terminal-automation partner is named (sl.19).
