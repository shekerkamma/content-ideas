## Current level: 0–1

DeepGrid has unusually good raw material and no operating layer. The July deck contains a
real ICP scorecard, a two-track positioning statement with kill criteria, and a banned-claims
list — genuinely Level-3-grade thinking that has never been written into files anything can
execute against.

## Why the documents cannot serve as the Brain

- **They contradict each other.** 14 mapped contradictions between the IM, the July deck and
  BP-1A, including gross margin, die area, defence revenue and the mandate date.
- **They are not addressable.** "Read BP-1A §7" is not a context file; it is a research task.
- **They contain retired claims.** Six of the nine claims retired by verification still sit
  in the June IM, which is the most polished and most circulated document.

## Next level: 3 — the Brain

Skipping 1 and 2 is permitted here under the "smallest missing artifact" rule: the six
context files are the prerequisite for any prompt or chain worth saving, and writing prompts
first would encode the contradictions. Level 1 and 2 artifacts become trivial once the Brain
exists.

## Calendar task this removes

Every outbound message, proposal and call prep currently requires a human to reconcile four
disagreeing documents and remember which claims are retired. The Brain moves that
reconciliation from a per-message task to a one-time write.

## Gate for this build

A cold chat, given only the six files and no other context, produces a defensible outbound
email to a named segment that a founder would send with light edits — and refuses to make
any claim absent from `04-proof.md`.
