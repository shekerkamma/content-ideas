## Sample 1 — precision framed as respect

> I want you to have these dates precisely, because they are the difference between a US plan
> and a US hope.

The move: state *why* the precision matters, in a contrast pair. Not "please see the dates."

## Sample 2 — volunteer the damaging fact

> I would rather show you this than have you discover it. These are the ten companies Tracxn
> ranks as closest peers in autonomous trucking, with disclosed private funding.

Then a table of competitors far better funded than DeepGrid. The bad news is delivered first,
in full, with sources — before any argument that softens it.

## Sample 3 — restate their position better than they did

> Let me state your proposal as I now understand it, because it is cleaner than the version I
> had been arguing with myself about.

Steel-man before answering. Then a two-column table: WHAT YOU PROPOSED / MY ANSWER.

## Sample 4 — real credit, and naming the hole in your own argument

> You made a point in conversation that I think is the strongest strategic idea either of us
> has put on the table… I agree with it, I think it is better than anything I had written, and
> it fixes the one real hole in this document: everything up to §9 ends at "gated on Series A"
> with no American capital plan at all. So here is that plan, with the parts I would push back
> on marked as such.

Credit is specific and unqualified. The weakness in his own document is named outright.
Disagreement is flagged inline rather than buried.

## Sample 5 — agree with the part that deserves it, then evidence it

> Your instinct to skip the majors is the part I most agree with. Daimler, Paccar and Volvo
> have entrenched Tier-1 relationships, multi-year qualification programmes and no particular
> reason to take our call in 2028.

Concrete named reasons. No hedging adverbs.

## The voice, derived from the samples

| Move | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Lead with the uncomfortable fact** | "I would rather show you this than have you discover it" |
| **"I would rather X than Y"** | The recurring construction. Use it — it is the most characteristic sentence shape |
| **Contrast pairs** | "a US plan and a US hope" · "opportunity, not a retrofit market" |
| **Steel-man first** | Restate their position, better, before answering |
| **Mark your own disagreement** | "with the parts I would push back on marked as such" |
| **Label the evidence** | SOURCED / PLAN markers sit next to claims, in the table |
| **Flat refusal, no cushion** | "I am rejecting this too." · "one of them is mine and I am not going to move on it" |
| **Own the weakness** | Name the hole in your own document before the reader finds it |

**Sentence shape:** long analytical sentence carrying the reasoning, then a short flat one
carrying the judgement. Tables whenever there are more than two comparable things.

**Never:** flattery openers, hedging adverbs ("quite", "fairly", "somewhat"), enthusiasm as
a substitute for evidence, or a claim without its evidence state attached.

## Banned words and phrases — usable now

Derived from the July deck's own discipline and the retired-claims list.

### Never use — retired claims
- "39.3 TOPS measured" · "84% gross margin" · "88% blended margin"
- "12.9× cheaper" · "₹1 Cr defence revenue" · "mandate-ready"
- "ASIL-D" (as a present-tense property) · "transformer VLA on-chip"
- any insurance-premium or accident-reduction percentage
- "mandate live since April 2026"

### Never use — corporate filler
- "I wanted to reach out" · "circle back" · "touch base" · "synergies"
- "revolutionary" · "game-changing" · "world-class" · "cutting-edge"
- compliments about the prospect's company as an opener

### Structural rules
- Short sentences. Plain English.
- One ask per message.
- Never open a fleet conversation on price.
- State evidence level, not aspiration: "filed", not "patented"; "simulation", not
  "validated"; "a path", not "certified".
