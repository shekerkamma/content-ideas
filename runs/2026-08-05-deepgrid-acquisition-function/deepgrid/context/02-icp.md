# ICP

Status: draft

## The prioritisation test

**What does this buyer require us to already hold?** Segments needing only eligibility and a
working demo are sellable now. Everything else is gated on certification, an OEM
relationship, or a product that does not exist.

## Target now

**Government / PSU / defence fleets.** Foreign silicon excluded by procurement rule; GeM
access already demonstrated. Requires eligibility, not certification. Buying trigger:
sovereignty and audit exposure. Decision unit: programme officer, security officer,
operations lead, finance.

**Plant, yard and port operators — DESIGN-PARTNER TRACK, not a sales target.** Private land — no homologation gate at all (AIS-162/188 bind public-road vehicles, not
private-yard equipment). **FMVSS appeared here in an earlier draft; that is the US standard
and this segment is India-facing. Corrected — if a US terminal is ever targeted, the whole
indigenous-supply and sovereign-data argument has to be rebuilt.**
Own their vehicles and their land. Buying trigger: throughput and incident cost. These become
the Phase-3 autonomy customers.

> **Unresolved prerequisite.** This segment wants a site reference and none exists. Do not
> pretend otherwise. Approach the first two accounts as **design partners** — instrument a
> site, agree the measure (moves per hour, near-misses), and trade influence over the product
> for access. The first signed site becomes the reference that opens the rest of the segment.

## Validate, do not forecast

**Large organised fleets (50+ trucks).** Real safety P&L, single decision-maker, capacity to
run a pilot. Requires certified AEBS, which does not exist until 2027. Treat as design
partners, not customers.

## Start now, closes late

**OEM / Tier-1 line-fit.** One relationship covers thousands of vehicles — the volume
engine. Requires homologation and a relationship DeepGrid does not have. Historical
fabless-to-design-in cycles run 5–8 years with 2–3 years of relationship first. It will
never be too early; it can easily be too late.

## Disqualifiers — these matter more than the qualifiers

- **Sub-five-truck owner-operators.** ~3.5M operators, no purchasing process, hard price
  anchor at ₹4,500–11,000. Not addressable directly at any sane acquisition cost, and the
  cheaper SKU that would reach them has never been specified or costed.
- **Anyone buying purely to pass inspection.** They will buy a ₹15,000–40,000 compliance box
  and they are right to. We lose on their terms.
- **3PL and freight aggregators, for now.** They aggregate small operators and negotiate on
  price leverage we cannot absorb pre-tapeout.
- **Buyers who need certification today.** Saying "mandate-ready" to close them is a banned
  claim and will not survive their evaluator.
- **Anyone requiring a quantified insurance or accident-reduction outcome.** We have no
  actuarial partner and no baseline. Walk rather than invent one.

## The timing fact that changes urgency

The ADAS perception mandate binds **1 Oct 2027** (new) and **1 Jan 2028** (existing), under
G.S.R. 834(E) of Nov 2025. AEBS binds 1 Jan 2027 / 1 Oct 2027. GSR 184(E) was a draft. Any
pitch built on a 2026 deadline is wrong and a well-read buyer will know.

## OPEN — two questions that block any bus or PSU road-fleet approach

**1. Does the perception mandate bind buses?** G.S.R. 834(E) dates are recorded here without a
vehicle category. Every worked example in these files is N2/N3 trucks; a bus is M3. **No
source states whether M3 is in scope.** Do not quote a mandate date to a bus operator until
this is confirmed — this file's own rule is that a wrong deadline gets caught by a well-read
buyer, and it costs the credibility of every true claim alongside it.

**2. Is AD0 road-legal?** AD0 replaces statutory mirrors with cameras on a public-road
vehicle. "Eligibility, not certification, is the gate" is about *procurement* eligibility, not
about whether a camera-monitor system replacing mirrors needs its own type approval. If it
does, the sellable-today thesis has a hole in it. Nothing in any source addresses this.
