# Full Strategy: Title, Escrow & Closing Workflow Automation

## Strategic thesis

Title and escrow workflow automation is worth pursuing when it is sold as transaction coordination and closing throughput, not as a generic AI assistant.

## Why the wedge works

- The workflow is multi-party, document-heavy, and exception-prone.
- Buyers already accept workflow software and process automation.
- Missing items, delay, and follow-up burden create visible operational cost.

## Ideal entry point

1. Ingest order data and opening details.
2. Apply workflow templates, roles, and checklist logic.
3. Surface missing items and route reminders or escalation tasks.
4. Expand into funding, vendor, document, and underwriter coordination.

## Solution-stack recommendation

- Title/escrow and closing platforms remain the systems of action.
- OpenHands orchestrates file setup, follow-up, and exception workflow agents.
- Docusign-style document systems handle agreement and signature state.
- Reporting focuses on file-opening speed, missing-item backlog, exception queues, and closing readiness.

## Delivery model

- Phase 1: file setup and checklist automation
- Phase 2: exception routing and partner coordination
- Phase 3: broader connected-transaction workflow packaging

## Risks

- Bad routing or checklist logic slows closings
- Security and privacy expectations are high
- Partner responsiveness may still bottleneck throughput
- Teams may distrust opaque automation in sensitive workflows

## Controls

- Keep sensitive approvals and releases human-controlled
- Encode explicit checklist and exception rules
- Start with one queue or one team before broader rollout
- Keep the workflow and audit trail visible at each step

## Recommended account angle

Target title and escrow operators or real-estate transaction teams with enough file volume that coordination friction is already painful. The best pitch is faster closing readiness with cleaner stakeholder orchestration.
