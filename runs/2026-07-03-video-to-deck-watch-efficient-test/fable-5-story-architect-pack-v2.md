# Fable 5 Story Architect Pack V2

## BLUF

Fable 5 should be reserved for high-context work that compounds: improving reusable skills, diagnosing systems with recurring failures, and planning complex features where scattered context would otherwise produce the wrong implementation.

## Audience Decision

Decide which few assets deserve the remaining Fable window, then convert the output into durable skills, specs, and implementation plans rather than treating it as a one-off chat.

## Tension

The access window is short and the model becomes expensive afterward. Random experimentation may be interesting, but it does not create reusable leverage. The risk is wasting the model on disposable outputs while the work that shapes daily execution, product reliability, and complex architecture remains unimproved.

## Argument Arc

1. Scarce access forces a choice between throwaway experimentation and durable leverage.
2. Fable's advantage is preserving intent across context, details, and edge cases.
3. The first durable asset is the skill library because it shapes repeated work.
4. The skill demo shows that strong processes can still fail when detail and tool handoffs break.
5. The second durable asset is the existing product feature because repeated bug fixes can hide a deeper system issue.
6. The product demo shows that the agent loop needed state persistence and server-side orchestration, not more patching.
7. The third durable asset is complex feature planning because code, notes, product intent, and research need to be merged.
8. The model comparison shows why plausible plans can still be dangerous when they reintroduce bugs, mishandle interrupts, or misunderstand platform capability.

## Evidence Map

Direct evidence:
- Fable is briefly available and likely expensive after the window.
- The video recommends improving skills, reviewing existing features, and planning ambitious features.
- The skill-library demo found downstream detail loss and missing tool-specific adapters.
- The product demo identified a stateless server-side loop and overreliance on client-side orchestration.
- The planning demo includes token streaming, server event streaming, and front-end rendering.
- The comparison identifies risks including reintroduced bugs, broken interruption flow, wrong platform assumptions, and missed code areas.

Fair synthesis:
- Fable should be treated as a high-context planning layer.
- The high-cost model should create plans and specs that cheaper models can implement.
- The three use cases are best understood as compounding asset work.

Interpretation to keep softened:
- "Fable is better" should be framed through the speaker's demonstrated examples, not as an independent benchmark.
- "Dangerous plans" should be tied to the specific risk classes described in the demo.
