# Agent Replacement Scorecard: Executive Decision Memo

## Recommendation
Approve a 30-day AI agent software-portfolio diagnostic to identify which workflow layers can be replaced, which SaaS modules should be renegotiated, and which governed systems of record should be protected. The diagnostic should focus on workflows, not SaaS categories, because agent pressure lands first on execution layers: seats, add-ons, runbooks, assisted workflows, drafting, extraction, support, and guided selling.

## Decision Required
Approve the diagnostic as a client-facing offer and use the scorecard as the workshop operating model. The output should be a portfolio view with three actions: replace, renegotiate, keep.

## Context
Executives are hearing broad claims that AI agents will replace SaaS. That framing is too crude for real decisions. Most enterprise platforms combine exposed workflow layers with durable control or data substrates. Replacing the wrong layer creates operational and governance risk; failing to challenge exposed modules leaves renewal leverage unused.

## Options Considered

| Option | Upside | Trade-Off | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wait for vendors to repackage AI | Low effort; avoids premature action | Vendor controls economics; renewal leverage is lost | Reject |
| Run isolated agent pilots | Fast learning; easy to launch | Pilots stay disconnected from SaaS spend and renewal decisions | Partial |
| Run portfolio diagnostic | Links AI capability to spend, renewal, and risk decisions | Requires workflow inventory and stakeholder scoring | Recommend |

## Evidence
- The current scorecard has 25 scored use cases across 6 agent domains.
- The evidence base separates into 10 replace, 9 renegotiate, and 6 keep cases.
- Data moat is the cleanest decision boundary: low moat maps to replace, medium moat maps to renegotiate, high moat maps to keep.
- Customer workflows skew toward replacement; security workflows skew toward durability; employee and data workflows are mostly renegotiation opportunities.

## Risks and Mitigations
- **Risk: The evidence base is curated, not statistically sampled.** Mitigation: present the scorecard as a diagnostic, not market proof.
- **Risk: Executives challenge named proof examples.** Mitigation: add source URLs and proof-status labels before external distribution.
- **Risk: The deck overstates replacement.** Mitigation: make "keep" and "renegotiate" equal first-class outcomes.
- **Risk: The client lacks spend data.** Mitigation: use the diagnostic to collect module, seat, renewal, and workflow-volume data.
