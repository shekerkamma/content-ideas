# Competitive investor brief

## Executive answer

The portfolio is not ten alternatives to NVIDIA. It contains three different economic games: infrastructure interfaces that can attach to multiple winners, flexible compute platforms that must build software ecosystems, and narrow accelerators that monetize only if their target workload remains stable. The most defensible diligence priority is broad-attachment infrastructure plus shipping systems; the highest upside and highest evidence gap sit together in Etched, MatX and Fractile.

## Arenas and real competitors

- Optical interconnect: Lightmatter competes with Ayar Labs, Celestial AI, Broadcom/TSMC packaging ecosystems and incumbent pluggable optics—not with Tenstorrent.
- Scale-out fabric: Cornelis faces NVIDIA InfiniBand, Ethernet/Ultra Ethernet, HPE Slingshot and hyperscaler network design.
- DPU/switching: Xsight faces Broadcom, NVIDIA, Marvell and custom hyperscaler silicon.
- Open compute/IP: Tenstorrent faces NVIDIA/AMD systems, RISC-V and Arm IP, Cerebras and hyperscaler custom accelerators.
- Inference silicon: d-Matrix faces Groq-class low-latency systems, Positron, FuriosaAI, Rebellions, SambaNova and NVIDIA inference stacks.
- Edge inference: Axelera faces Hailo, SiMa.ai, Kneron, Qualcomm and NVIDIA Jetson.
- Reconfigurable HPC: NextSilicon faces NVIDIA/AMD accelerators, Intel CPUs/GPUs, FPGAs and national-lab procurement inertia.
- Specialist frontier inference: Etched, MatX and Fractile face one another only partially; their more dangerous competitors are evolving NVIDIA systems, TPU/Trainium/Maia, Broadcom-designed ASICs and changing model architectures.

## Investor posture

Advance Lightmatter, Cornelis, Xsight, Tenstorrent, d-Matrix and NextSilicon into differentiated diligence tracks. Keep Axelera as an edge-specific execution candidate. Treat Etched, MatX and Fractile as milestone-priced options until independent workload economics and repeatable systems deployment exist. Do not infer diversification from company count: the accelerator names share foundry, HBM, packaging, software migration, hyperscaler procurement and model-drift risks.
