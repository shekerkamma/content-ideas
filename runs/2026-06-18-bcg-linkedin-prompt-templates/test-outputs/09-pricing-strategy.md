# Pricing Strategy — Apex HR

## Pricing Power Diagnosis
Current model: per-seat SaaS, flat module tiers. Estimated discount rate: 25–35% in competitive deals. No payroll = no anchor for higher ACV.

## Willingness-to-Pay Analysis
| Segment | Current ACV | Estimated WTP | Gap | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Growth-Stage Scaler (150–300) | $16K | $22–28K | +$6–12K | Payroll bundle would justify premium |
| Stable Mid-Market (300–500) | $28K | $35–45K | +$7–17K | Deep HCM + payroll + analytics bundle |
| Transitional SMB (50–150) | $7K | $7–9K | Minimal | Price-sensitive; WTP ceiling low |

## Discount Leakage Map
| Where Discounting Occurs | % of Deals | Revenue at Risk | Root Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitive RFPs vs Rippling | ~40% of deals | ~$3.2M ARR | No payroll = partial solution = forced discount to close |
| Renewal pressure from growing accounts | ~25% of accounts | ~$2.1M ARR | No upsell path = champion demands discount to stay |
| SMB self-serve trials | ~15% of accounts | ~$0.8M ARR | No value metric anchor; discount habit formed at entry |

## Recommendations
1. Launch payroll as a premium tier at +$4/employee/month — removes justification for competitive discount
2. Move to value-based pricing (per employee) rather than flat seat pricing — aligns price to customer growth
3. Introduce Analytics as a standalone $3K–5K/year add-on — 85% gross margin, currently bundled away
4. Set minimum contract floor at $12K for 150+ employee accounts — stops SMB pricing bleeding into mid-market
5. Pre-announce payroll pricing to existing customers to lock renewals before competitive alternatives close in
