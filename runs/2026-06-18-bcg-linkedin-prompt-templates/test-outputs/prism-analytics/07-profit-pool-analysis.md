# Profit Pool Analysis — Prism Analytics

## Scope
Financial Analytics and Risk Intelligence SaaS market for North American banks, insurers, and asset managers. Value chain analyzed: raw financial data provision → platform software → regulatory compliance interpretation and reporting → analytics and risk intelligence → AI-augmented workflow automation. Prism's current position: platform software + regulatory compliance reporting. Products in scope: Basel III/IV compliance automation, CECL provisioning engines, IFRS 9 credit risk analytics, portfolio risk dashboards, and the forthcoming LLM natural language interface. Customer segments in scope: Tier 1 banks, regional banks, insurance, wealth management. Time horizon: FY2025 actuals + FY2026–2027 projection.

## Value Capture
| Player or Activity | Value Captured | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Bloomberg Terminal (data + analytics) | ~40–45% of total market value captured | Unmatched real-time data moat; dual pricing power on data subscriptions and analytics seats; brand lock-in with traders and risk officers; no viable substitution for terminal-dependent workflows |
| Clearwater Analytics (AI-native platform) | ~8–10% of market value captured, rapidly expanding | First-mover advantage in AI-native architecture; high growth (55%) compounding annual market share; capturing disproportionate share of new budget created by AI-native demand; will likely reach 15–18% capture within 3 years |
| SS&C Axiom (legacy installed base) | ~12–15% of market value captured, declining | Incumbency in regional and community banks provides captive renewal revenue despite poor product; value capture through renewal friction, not product quality |
| Prism Analytics | ~7–9% of total market value captured | Regulatory compliance depth in purpose-built CECL/Basel/IFRS 9 modules; Tier 1 bank relationships with high ACV; currently being squeezed between Bloomberg (top) and Clearwater (challenger) |
| Moody's Analytics (credit risk models) | ~6–8% of market value captured | Credit risk brand and proprietary economic scenario models; captures premium from banks that require Moody's PD/LGD data in their CECL calculations; model licensing is recurring and high-margin |
| Implementation and managed services (Big 4, boutique FinTech SIs) | ~10–12% of market value captured | Integration complexity (core banking, risk data warehouse, reporting) creates sustained SI value; Deloitte, EY, Accenture all have financial risk practices that capture margin on Prism and competitor implementations |

## Strategic Implications
1. **Where to play — double down on regulatory compliance SaaS, the highest-margin and most defensible pool**: The Basel/CECL/IFRS 9 compliance reporting pool has the best combination of margin (70–78%), growth durability (non-discretionary regulatory spend), and Prism's right to win (purpose-built, examiner-grade, 9 years of regulatory depth). This is where value capture is structurally protected by compliance mandate, multi-year contracts, and auditor validation requirements. Prism should invest to deepen this moat — additional regulatory standards (Basel IV final rules, DFAST stress testing automation, IFRS 17 for insurance) rather than competing in pools it cannot yet win.

2. **Where to avoid — do not attempt to compete in real-time portfolio risk analytics without a credible architecture plan**: The real-time analytics pool is growing at 20–25% CAGR, but Clearwater is the structural winner. Prism entering this pool with a T+1 architecture would consume $15M+ and 18+ months with uncertain competitive outcome. The profit pool here is real but the right to win requires foundational investment Prism cannot afford on current runway. Either make the strategic investment explicitly (with board commitment) or explicitly concede this pool and invest the capital elsewhere.

3. **How to improve capture — the LLM interface is a value capture mechanism, not just a product feature**: The AI-augmented financial intelligence pool ($80M today, $350M by 2028) is the fastest-growing pool Prism can enter with its current IP. Positioning the LLM interface as a regulatory-intelligence product (not a generic chatbot) captures margin from users who currently pay consultants and analysts to interpret regulatory reports. Every hour of analyst time replaced by a Prism LLM query is a monetization opportunity — consider usage-based pricing for LLM query volume as an expansion revenue layer on top of the base platform subscription.
