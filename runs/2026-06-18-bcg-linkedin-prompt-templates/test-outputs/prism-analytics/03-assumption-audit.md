# Assumption Audit — Prism Analytics

## Strategy Being Tested
Prism Analytics is pursuing a two-part growth recovery strategy: (1) launch a natural language LLM query interface in 6 months to reframe competitive positioning against AI-native peers like Clearwater Analytics, and (2) defend existing ARR through retention improvements and continued regulatory compliance depth while managing burn toward a board-defined outcome of either profitability or strategic sale within 18–24 months. The implicit assumption underlying both tracks is that the LLM interface can materially improve win rates and NRR before the runway forces a forced exit or down-round.

## Assumption Register
| Assumption | Category | Importance | Evidence Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The LLM interface will improve competitive win rates in deals where it was previously absent | Market / Customer | Critical | Weak — no live proof point; 6 months from launch; Clearwater already has AI features | High — if buyers view LLM interface as table stakes parity rather than differentiation, the win rate lift may be minimal |
| T+1 batch processing is not a disqualifier in the majority of Prism's target deals | Product | Critical | Contradicted — $1.2M TCV lost to Clearwater in part due to real-time gap; win rate dropped 16 points | High — if 50%+ of losses are real-time-related, LLM interface alone is insufficient |
| Regional bank segment is a defensible base with acceptable churn | Customer | High | Mixed — 6% gross churn rising; NRR declining; but Tier 1 banks show stronger retention | Medium-High — if churn migrates from small regionals to mid-size regionals, NRR could fall below 100% within 12–18 months |
| 18 months runway is sufficient to deliver the LLM interface and show measurable ARR impact | Operational | Critical | Shaky — 6 months to launch + 3–6 months to land first referenceable deals + board patience = 9–12 months of meaningful data, leaving 6–9 months of buffer | High — any launch delay, slow adoption, or board impatience compresses the window |
| A strategic sale is available at an acceptable multiple if the LLM story lands | Market / Strategic | High | Unvalidated — no confirmed inbound M&A interest stated; financial analytics M&A multiples depend heavily on growth rate | Medium — at 14% growth, strategic acquirers may price at 4–6× ARR ($192–$288M); at 25% growth, 7–9× ARR ($336–$432M); the multiple gap is meaningful |
| Engineering can deliver the LLM interface in 6 months with 30% of 90 engineers on the LLM layer | Operational | High | Reasonable but untested — 27 engineers on LLM layer is a credible allocation; 6-month SaaS product launches are achievable but carry schedule risk | Medium — 30% reallocation to LLM while maintaining platform stability is achievable but not low-risk |
| Clearwater does not materially improve its regulatory compliance depth before Prism launches LLM interface | Competitive | High | Uncontrolled — Clearwater has capital ($180M ARR, likely $250M+ valuation) and growth momentum to invest in compliance | Medium-High — if Clearwater closes the Basel/CECL/IFRS 9 gap, Prism loses its last differentiation moat |
| Tier 1 bank customers (32% of base) are sticky and not vulnerable to Clearwater displacement | Customer | High | Partially supported — NRR 104% is aggregate; Tier 1 stickiness likely higher due to switching costs; but Clearwater lost deal was likely Tier 1 | Medium — confirmation bias risk; one $1.2M loss suggests Tier 1s are not immune |
| Reducing burn to profitability is achievable without cutting R&D investment in the LLM interface | Financial | Medium | Uncertain — $2.1M/mo burn at 74% gross margin on $48M ARR implies ~$26M in OpEx; reducing to profitability requires material headcount or G&A cuts | Medium-High — cutting to profitability while accelerating product is inherently contradictory; tradeoffs have not been stated |

## Load-Bearing Assumptions
1. **The LLM interface will provide a credible differentiation narrative versus Clearwater** — this is the central bet. If buyers view the LLM interface as catching up to a capability Clearwater already delivers rather than advancing Prism's unique regulatory depth, the reframe fails. The entire near-term growth recovery depends on this assumption. If wrong, Prism is left defending a shrinking compliance reporting niche with no growth story, making a strategic sale more likely but at a distressed multiple.

2. **T+1 processing is not the primary win rate driver** — if investigation reveals that 60%+ of competitive losses cite real-time as the deciding factor (not AI UX or analytics breadth), then the LLM interface investment is solving the wrong problem. The board would then face a harder choice: a $15M+ replatform investment (consuming most of the remaining runway) or a forced exit. This assumption has the highest consequence if wrong.

3. **18 months is enough time to generate measurable LLM-driven ARR and demonstrate trajectory to the board** — if launch slips to 9+ months or early adoption is slow, the board review window for "profitability OR sale" arrives before results are in. This assumption is operationally load-bearing because it sets the tempo for every other decision.

## Test Plan
| Assumption | Test | Data Needed | Owner | Decision Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LLM interface drives win rate improvement | Run 10 LLM-interface-led pilot deals at beta launch; track win rate vs. matched historical control (same segment, ACV range) | Win/loss data segmented by presence of LLM demo in sales process; buyer stated reason for selection | VP Sales + Product | If LLM-enabled win rate exceeds 40% (vs 31% baseline) in 90 days post-launch, proceed; if <35%, reassess positioning |
| T+1 is not the primary loss driver | Structured win/loss interviews: pull last 40 competitive losses, code reasons (real-time, AI, UX, price, reputation); quantify share | Exit interview data, CRM deal closure notes, direct post-loss buyer calls | Sales Ops + CEO | If >50% of losses cite real-time as primary reason, escalate replatforming decision to board; if <30%, proceed with LLM-first strategy |
| Regional bank base is defensible | Cohort churn model: separate churn rate for accounts <$150K ACV vs $150–$350K ACV vs >$350K ACV over 8 quarters | Account-level ARR, churn date, stated reason | Customer Success | If churn rate in $150–$350K cohort exceeds 8% annualized, initiate emergency retention program and re-underwrite NRR forecast |
| Strategic sale is achievable at target multiple | Soft-circuit board advisors and known strategic acquirers (SS&C, FIS, Moody's) for informal appetite signals; model transaction at 5×, 7×, and 9× ARR scenarios | Inbound M&A interest, comparable transaction multiples (2024–2025 FinTech analytics M&A comps), growth rate sensitivity | CEO + Board | If no credible acquirer interest at >6× ARR within 6 months, deprioritize sale track and focus on profitability |
| Engineering delivers LLM interface in 6 months | Monthly milestone review against launch checklist; scope lock at Month 2; beta at Month 5 | Engineering sprint velocity, scope change log, beta client commitment | CTO | If launch slips past Month 7, trigger board notification and revise runway model |

## Recommendation
**Test first, then proceed with conditional investment.** The LLM interface bet is directionally correct — regulatory compliance depth + conversational UX is a defensible differentiation lane — but two critical unknowns must be resolved before the board review: (1) the primary reason for win rate collapse (real-time vs. AI breadth vs. other), and (2) whether the LLM interface produces measurable win rate lift in beta deals. Conduct structured win/loss analysis within 30 days (the data already exists in CRM). If real-time is the binding constraint, bring the replatforming decision to the board immediately with a clear build-buy-partner options analysis. If AI breadth / UX is the constraint, proceed with full LLM launch investment and use the launch as the M&A narrative anchor.
