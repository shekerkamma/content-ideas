# Executive Narrative — Prism Analytics

## Audience and Decision

**Audience:** Prism Analytics Board of Directors (Series C investors + independent directors); secondarily, the CEO who must carry this story into the room and own the recommendation.

**Decision required:** Approve $3.2M investment to accelerate LLM interface launch by 2 months and hire 4 sales engineers — structured as two tranches with a milestone hold trigger at Q3 2026. Defer real-time replatforming until ARR reaches $65M.

**Desired action:** Board votes to approve Tranche 1 ($1.8M) by end of June 2026, with Tranche 2 ($1.4M) contingent on hitting the Q3 LLM milestone.

## Core Message

Prism Analytics has the right product, the right customers, and the right market — but a narrowing window to close one specific AI perception gap before Clearwater locks in the compliance analytics category.

## Pyramid Logic

**Recommendation:** Invest $3.2M to accelerate the LLM interface and deploy 4 sales engineers. Defer replatforming to real-time until $65M ARR. Target $62M ARR by end of FY2026.

**Supporting argument 1 — The problem is specific and fixable.**
Win/loss analysis confirms that the AI perception gap — not compliance functionality, not pricing, not customer success — is the primary disqualifier in competitive losses. Prism's Basel IV and CECL depth is a genuine competitive advantage. The gap is UX and narrative, not product architecture. The LLM interface closes it.
- Evidence: $1.2M TCV deal lost to Clearwater 3 months ago; 16-point win-rate decline over 24 months; Clearwater at 55% growth vs. Prism's 14% are directionally correlated with Clearwater's AI-first positioning.

**Supporting argument 2 — The investment is structurally bounded and the return is asymmetric.**
At 74% gross margin, $14M in net new ARR generates $10.4M in gross profit. The $3.2M investment pays back in under 4 months of incremental gross profit. The downside — LLM ships late — is mitigated by the tranche structure: if the Q3 milestone is missed, Tranche 2 is held and the strategy is reassessed before further capital is deployed.
- Evidence: 4.4x gross return on investment; tranche hold trigger limits downside to $1.8M; break-even at 6-point win rate improvement on $28M pipeline.

**Supporting argument 3 — The alternative paths are worse.**
Replatforming to real-time now requires 18+ months and $15M+ — longer than the current runway. Doing nothing guarantees continued deceleration and a distressed sale. Raising a Series D at 14% growth means a flat or down round. The LLM acceleration is the only option that restores the growth narrative and the valuation story before any capital event.
- Evidence: Real-time replatforming estimate = 18 months + $15M+; current runway = 18 months at $2.1M/mo; Series C raised at implied 2022 valuation likely 40%+ above current growth-rate comps.

## Hostile Questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "You've been missing targets for two years. Why should we believe the win-rate can go from 31% to 42%?" | Because for the first time, we know exactly why we're losing. The prior decelerations were market headwinds and competitive gaps we couldn't name. Today we can name them: no AI interface. The LLM closes that gap. Three Tier 1 bank design partners have already agreed to early access — that's customer pull, not internal optimism. |
| "What if the LLM interface doesn't meet bank compliance accuracy standards?" | We have a hard UAT gate before any commercial announcement — 92% accuracy vs. a validated Basel IV / CECL query test suite, reviewed by three bank compliance officers. If we miss the gate, we delay GA rather than ship a non-compliant product. The design partner program exists precisely to surface this risk before it becomes a market risk. |
| "Why not just raise a Series D and replatform properly?" | A Series D at 14% growth is a flat or down round from the 2022 Series C. We would be diluting to fix a problem we can fix for $3.2M without dilution. Replatforming at $48M ARR also carries existential execution risk — 18 months of near-total engineering focus while Clearwater continues to compound. The sequence matters: restore growth rate first, then replatform from a position of strength. |
| "Is $3.2M enough — or will you be back asking for more?" | The tranche structure is designed specifically to answer this. We hold $1.4M of the $3.2M behind a Q3 milestone. If engineering slips, we do not release Tranche 2. If we need more than $3.2M, we surface it at the Tranche 2 decision point, not at a surprise Board meeting. The ask is bounded by design. |
| "What happens if we miss $62M ARR?" | We have two pre-committed responses built into the plan. If Q3 trajectory falls below $54M: option one, we activate the strategic sale process with a data room built on LLM GA and NRR recovery evidence — acquirers like Moody's or S&P Global would pay 4–5x forward ARR for what we will have built. Option two, we restructure to $1.7M/mo burn and extend runway to 22+ months. Neither outcome requires improvisation. |
| "Why not sell now before the burn gets worse?" | Today's story — 14% growth, no AI — is the weakest version of this company. In 12 months, with LLM GA, NRR recovery, and $62M ARR, the story is: fastest-growing AI compliance platform in Basel IV / CECL. That story commands a materially higher multiple. We are one strategic cycle away from a premium exit. |
