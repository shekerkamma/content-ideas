# Executive summary note — DeepGrid India ADAS competitive strategy

## Executive gist

India's commercial-vehicle ADAS market is no longer an open field. Domestic specialists
already possess credible local narratives, while global Tier-1 suppliers control OEM
integration, braking, homologation, and production accountability.

DeepGrid should not position itself as a complete AEBS replacement. Its strongest role is as
a low-cost, India-trained vision-perception subsystem embedded within radar-led platforms,
braking Tier-1 systems, and OEM safety programmes.

## What the competitive evidence means

- **Starkenn** is the most immediate domestic field competitor and a potential radar-fusion
  partner. It leads in mining, government fleets, and retrofit deployment.
- **Gahan AI** is the indigenous radar challenger to monitor, but its qualification and
  production claims require primary verification.
- **drivebuddyAI** has a credible DMS, fleet-intelligence, and India-road-data proposition;
  its ownership of the AEBS braking boundary remains unproven.
- **ZF and Aptiv** are the strongest OEM threats because they control integration,
  validation, and programme execution—not merely perception technology.
- **STRADVISION** is the closest substitute at DeepGrid's software layer and must be
  benchmarked on identical hardware and Indian operating conditions.
- **Sterling–MINIEYE** has a credible local-market route, although the partnership has not
  yet demonstrated an Indian production nomination.
- **bitsensing and Netrasemi** are adjacent benchmarks: one validates retrofit demand; the
  other represents a future indigenous-compute alternative.
- **Incumbent Tier-1s** remain the structural gatekeepers. DeepGrid should attach to their
  open subsystem boundaries rather than attempt to displace their complete stacks.

## Recommended strategic posture

1. **Partner and attach** where competitors control radar, braking, or OEM programme
   accountability.
2. **Compete selectively** in mining, government, and fleet opportunities where DeepGrid can
   prove superior VRU classification, local adaptation, and lifecycle economics.
3. **Benchmark rigorously** against Gahan AI and STRADVISION using the same hardware, Indian
   ODD, and measurable performance criteria.
4. **Build field evidence quickly** through paid, instrumented pilots with telemetry and
   publishable deployment metrics.
5. **Lead with buyer economics and bounded integration**, not semiconductor ownership alone.

## Core message

> DeepGrid does not need to own the entire vehicle-safety system to win. It must become the
> most economical, India-ready, and integration-friendly vision-perception layer inside the
> systems that already own radar, braking, and homologation.
