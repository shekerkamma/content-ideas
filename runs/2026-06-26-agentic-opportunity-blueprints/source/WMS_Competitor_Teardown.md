# Competitor Teardown: Warehouse & Inventory Management (Use Case 27)

## 1. Company Overview
**Top Legacy Incumbents:** Manhattan Associates, Blue Yonder
**Primary Positioning:** Premier enterprise supply chain execution and warehouse management systems (WMS).

## 2. Product Teardown
- **Top 3 Features:** Omnichannel fulfillment, AI-driven demand planning, complex facility execution.
- **Pricing:** Highly customized RFPs. Typically requires a "seven-figure" investment based on facility count and SKU volume. No public pricing.
- **Onboarding Friction:** Extremely high. Deployment is a massive pain point. These are not plug-and-play solutions and require heavy external partner support and intensive training cycles.

## 3. Where They Are Strong
- Handling deep, complex omnichannel logistics.
- Highly scalable for massive enterprise distribution networks.
- Partner networks and ecosystem dominance.

## 4. Where They Are Weak (The "Human Middleware" Problem)
- **Steep Learning Curve:** Configuration and daily operations require extensive manual training.
- **Restrictive Modifications:** While feature-rich, modifying the software to fit unique warehouse processes is rigid, creating operational bottlenecks.
- **High TCO:** The total cost of ownership extends far beyond software due to reliance on IT consultants to make basic operational adjustments.

## 5. Disruptive Strategy (Our Agentic Wedge)
- **Top 2 Direct Threats:** Manhattan Associates, Blue Yonder.
- **Table Stakes:** Inventory tracking, location logic, replenishment signaling.
- **What We Must NOT Do:** Do NOT replace the physical warehouse hardware or system of record databases. Orchestrate the intelligence layer above it.
- **3 Specific Gaps We Exploit:**
  1. *Seven-figure entry barrier vs. Low-cost, high-ROI Agent-led WMS layers.*
  2. *Restrictive rigid configurations vs. Adaptive Agentic logic that learns facility-specific workflows.*
  3. *Massive manual training overhead vs. Conversational UI where workers just "ask" the system where things are.*
