# Competitor Teardown: FAQ / KB / Deflection

**Target SaaS Incumbents:** Document360, Confluence, Guru, Helpjuice.

**Company Overview:** 
These tools focus on internal and external knowledge management, aiming to deflect tickets by providing searchable documentation. 

**Product Teardown & Pricing:**
*   **Confluence:** ~$5 to $10/user/mo for Standard/Premium.
*   **Guru:** ~$25/user/mo for AI-focused delivery.
*   **Helpjuice:** Tiered flat fee (~$249 to $799/month).
*   **Document360:** Opaque quote-based enterprise pricing.

**Where They Are Strong:**
Content governance, deep integrations into Slack/Teams (Guru), and structured article formatting.

**Where They Are Weak (Onboarding Friction):**
The fatal flaw is the human maintenance tax. As one user noted, "Maintaining the documentation is a bigger problem than creating it." Companies pay high per-user fees to access knowledge, but the knowledge rots quickly.

**Disruptive Strategy:**
*   **Direct Threats:** Guru and Document360.
*   **Table Stakes:** Searchability, access control, WYSIWYG formatting.
*   **What NOT to do:** Do not build just another Markdown editor or WYSIWYG wiki.
*   **The Agentic Wedge:** *Eliminate the maintenance tax.* Instead of asking users to manually write and update FAQs, the agent autonomously ingests Slack conversations, resolved tickets, and raw docs to auto-generate and auto-update the KB on the fly. The agent serves the answer directly to the customer, rendering the static "Help Center" obsolete.
