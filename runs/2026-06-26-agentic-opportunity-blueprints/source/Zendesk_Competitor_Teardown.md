# Competitor Teardown: Per-Seat Help Desks

**Target SaaS Incumbents:** Zendesk, Intercom, Freshdesk, ServiceNow, Jira Service Management (JSM).

**Company Overview:** 
These platforms dominate the traditional "System of Record" customer support market, built on a per-seat licensing model designed for human agents. They are the established middleware for customer operations.

**Product Teardown & Pricing:**
*   **Zendesk:** Advertises ~$19/agent/mo, but true omnichannel/analytics require $55 to $115/agent/mo. AI "Copilot" features cost an additional ~$50/mo per agent.
*   **Intercom:** Essential starts at ~$29/seat/mo, Advanced at ~$85/seat/mo. Their AI agent (Fin) charges $0.99 per resolution.
*   **ServiceNow / Enterprise:** Opaque, highly customized contracts with massive implementation costs (often 3-5x the first-year license fee).

**Where They Are Strong:**
Omnichannel routing, integrations, reporting, and being the entrenched system of record where historical customer data lives.

**Where They Are Weak (Onboarding Friction):**
Reddit users complain that Zendesk is a "heavy" platform requiring significant "ops brain" and full-time admins to maintain. Implementations are described as a "nightmare." They suffer from feature bloat, high per-seat taxes, and users hate paying for "enterprise features" they never use. The UI causes friction through constant context-switching.

**Disruptive Strategy:**
*   **Direct Threats:** Zendesk and Intercom.
*   **Table Stakes:** Ticket ingestion, omnichannel capability, basic routing.
*   **What NOT to do:** Do not build a new database or system of record.
*   **The Agentic Wedge:** *Collapse the seat.* We sit on top of their messy data and resolve tickets autonomously for cents. We bypass the $100K/9-month setup model by requiring zero rigid workflow configuration—the agent reads the historical tickets and acts natively, replacing the human middleware without ripping out the underlying database.
