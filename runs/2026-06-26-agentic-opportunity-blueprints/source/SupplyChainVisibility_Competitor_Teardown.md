# Competitor Teardown: Supply Chain Visibility & Risk (Use Case 29)

## 1. Company Overview
**Top Legacy Incumbents:** Project44, FourKites
**Primary Positioning:** Real-time transportation visibility platforms (RTTVP) and supply chain risk monitoring.

## 2. Product Teardown
- **Top 3 Features:** Multimodal real-time tracking, dynamic yard management, geofencing.
- **Pricing:** Custom, quote-based enterprise contracts. Prohibitively expensive barrier to entry for smaller LSPs.
- **Onboarding Friction:** Complex legacy integrations (connecting to varied ERP/TMS/WMS ecosystems) lead to long deployment timelines and reliance on professional services.

## 3. Where They Are Strong
- Unmatched visibility network density.
- Aggregation of massive amounts of carrier API/ELD data.

## 4. Where They Are Weak (The "Human Middleware" Problem)
- **Dashboard Fatigue:** Reddit users complain these tools just create "another dashboard." If it doesn't reduce manual emails/calls, human logistics coordinators still act as middleware to actually solve the freight issues.
- **Stale Data / False Positives:** Delayed notifications and geofencing inaccuracies lead to frustration when managing exceptions.
- **Inflexible Customization:** Limitations in tailoring the dashboards to unique operational workflows.

## 5. Disruptive Strategy (Our Agentic Wedge)
- **Top 2 Direct Threats:** Project44, FourKites.
- **Table Stakes:** Real-time location tracking, ETA predictions.
- **What We Must NOT Do:** Do NOT become just another alert dashboard. We must execute the resolution, not just notify.
- **3 Specific Gaps We Exploit:**
  1. *Alert fatigue vs. Autonomous Agent Firefighting (the agent actually emails the carrier to resolve the delay instead of just flashing red).*
  2. *Stale tracking data vs. Real-time LLM reasoning that ingests multimodal context (weather, traffic, emails).*
  3. *Expensive enterprise contracts vs. Pay-per-incident or usage-based pricing for mid-market shippers.*
