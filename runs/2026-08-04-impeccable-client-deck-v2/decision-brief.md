# Impeccable 4.0: adoption decision brief

## Executive summary

Impeccable's strongest demonstrated value is not superior autonomous generation. It introduces a
human-steered choice architecture that separates macro direction, within-direction variation,
local refinement, and independent challenge. This can reduce wrong-direction rework—but only when
the organization supplies evidence discipline, named decision owners, change-routing rules, and
conventional technical QA.

## Findings

1. **The mechanism is structured divergence followed by visual convergence.** Worlds externalize
   the macro taste decision; variants narrow execution; Live Mode supports bounded local choices.
2. **Generality creates a real operating trade-off.** Range and customization increase, but so do
   operator variance, decision burden, and governance requirements.
3. **Change altitude is the critical routing rule.** Local problems belong in Live Mode; structural
   problems belong in the terminal; wrong directions require a reference reset.
4. **Taste becomes reusable only through memory and challenge.** DESIGN.md, detection, and a
   fresh-context review can reduce drift, but none independently proves accessibility, factual
   accuracy, performance, usability, or business value.
5. **The supplied demonstration reaches structured convergence, not measured optimization.** It
   shows roughly maturity Level 2 and pieces of Level 3; no supplied data supports Levels 4–5.

## Recommendation

Pilot Impeccable on one bounded landing-page workflow. Keep the brief, real evidence, references,
stack, model budget, and acceptance criteria fixed. Compare against the current process using blind
quality review and representative-user testing. Expand only if major redesign cycles and approval
time fall without regressions in accessibility, responsiveness, performance, factual integrity,
reference provenance, or cost.

## Confidence

Medium. The operating mechanisms are clearly demonstrated and several trade-offs are explicitly
acknowledged by the presenter. Productivity, design-quality, usability, and commercial benefits
remain hypotheses requiring a controlled pilot.
