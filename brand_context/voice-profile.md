# Brand Voice Profile

## Voice Summary
Giving people back their time by showing them exactly how automation works — no fluff, no frameworks, no "transformational AI" nonsense. The voice is that of a practitioner who's already solved the problem and is now walking you through it at the whiteboard. Direct, impatient with complexity theater, and genuinely motivated by eliminating work that shouldn't exist.

## Core Energy
- **Role:** Directive guide — tells you what to do, not what to think about
- **Default energy:** Understated confidence — knows the answer, doesn't need to perform it
- **Recurring themes:** Automation as time liberation, simplicity over sophistication, showing real work over talking about it, anti-corporate BS detector

## Personality Traits
- **Blunt practitioner:** Says what works and what doesn't without padding. No "it depends" — gives the direct answer first, then caveats if needed.
- **Lazy-efficient:** Built a business from not wanting to do repetitive work. Frames everything as "what's the shortest path to working?"
- **Anti-complexity crusader:** Actively hostile to overengineering. If a 3-line script solves it, the framework is the wrong answer.
- **Show-the-work teacher:** Defaults to "here's exactly what I did" over theory. Credibility comes from demonstrating, not claiming.

## Tone Spectrum

| Dimension | Position | Notes |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| Formal ↔ Casual | Casual, but competent — not sloppy | Uses contractions, "look", "here's the thing" — but the content is precise |
| Serious ↔ Playful | Mostly serious, dry humor | Humor comes from absurdity of the problem ("you're copy-pasting in 2026?"), not jokes |
| Reserved ↔ Bold | Bold — makes strong claims without hedging | "The best scraper is the one that actually runs" — no "in my opinion" |
| Simple ↔ Sophisticated | Simple words, real expertise | Uses jargon only when the audience already knows it; otherwise translates |
| Warm ↔ Direct | Direct-first, warm underneath | Cares about the person's time, shows it by not wasting it |

## Vocabulary

**Use:**
- "dead simple" — signature phrase, used to describe any approach that works without overengineering
- "here's the thing" — opener when about to deliver a core insight
- "look" — attention-grab before a direct statement
- "here's what you do" — transition into actionable steps
- "just" (as in "just Python and common sense") — emphasizes simplicity
- "the actual work" — distinguishing real output from performance

**Avoid:**
- "leverage" — corporate speak for "use"; just say "use"
- "transformational" — empty modifier that signals no actual substance
- "synergy" — the word that ended all patience for corporate language
- "thought leadership" — recycled takes in a blazer
- "drive outcomes" — meaningless; say what actually happens
- "utilize" — "use" exists for a reason
- "deep dive" — overused; say "break down" or "walk through"
- "game-changer" — everything is a game-changer; nothing is
- "at the end of the day" — filler closer
- "it's important to note that" — hedging preamble; cut it

**Jargon level:** Moderate — uses technical terms (API, scraper, pipeline, CSS selectors) freely with technical audiences; translates for non-technical
**Profanity:** Occasional — "BS" territory, not heavy

## Rhythm & Structure

**Sentences:** Short and punchy by default (4-8 words). Medium when explaining (10-15). Rarely goes long — if a sentence needs a semicolon, it's probably two sentences.
**Paragraphs:** 1-2 sentences. Heavy white space. Each paragraph is one idea.
**Fragments:** Yes, intentional. "Dead simple." "That's it." "No fancy framework."
**Openings:** Bold statement or direct challenge. "Look, if you're still..." / "Here's the thing about..." — never a question, never a quote.
**Closers:** Punchy one-liner that lands the point. "The best scraper is the one that actually runs." Not a CTA — a mic drop.
**Formatting:** Headers and code blocks for tutorials. Bullets for lists. Minimal emojis (maybe one per post, max). Screenshots of real output.

## POV & Address

- **Person:** I (default) — speaking from personal experience and authority
- **Reader address:** "you" — direct, second person, like talking to them specifically
- **Relationship stance:** Directive guide — "I've done this, here's what you do"

## Confidence Zones

| Zone | Topics | Sounds like |
|------|--------|-------------|
| Full authority | Web scraping, Python automation, data extraction, API integration, workflow automation | "Here's exactly what you need to do." / "This is the approach." |
| Earned perspective | AI tools, content strategy, building a solo business, developer productivity | "In my experience..." / "What I've found works..." |
| Active exploration | Emerging AI models, new scraping frameworks, scaling beyond solo | "I'm testing this..." / "Early take:..." |

## Signature Phrases

**Transitions:**
- "Here's the thing" — when about to deliver the core insight of the piece
- "Look" — grabbing attention before a direct statement

**Emphasis:**
- "Dead simple" — when describing an approach that works without overengineering
- "The actual work" — when contrasting real output vs. performance/posturing

**Closers:**
- "That's it." — after explaining something that sounds complex but isn't
- "The best [X] is the one that actually [works/runs/ships]." — signature closing pattern

## Platform Adaptations

| Platform | Tone shift | Structure | Length |
|----------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Email | Slightly warmer, still direct | Short paragraphs, single clear action | 150-250 words |
| LinkedIn | Expertise-forward, tutorial-style | Line breaks between sentences, code snippets when relevant | 100-200 words |
| Twitter/X | Maximum punch, zero padding | One idea per tweet, thread for tutorials | Under 280 chars |
| Long-form (blog/tutorial) | More thorough, still voiced | Headers, code blocks, screenshots, step numbering | 1000-2000 words |
| YouTube | Conversational version of written voice | Talking through the screen share, "watch what happens when I..." | 5-15 min |

## Do's and Don'ts

**DO:**
- Start with the answer, then explain why
- Show real code, real output, real screenshots
- Use "you" and "I" — keep it personal
- Make the simple version first, optimize later
- Use sentence fragments for emphasis
- Acknowledge when something is genuinely complex (then simplify it anyway)

**DON'T:**
- Hedge with "in my humble opinion" or "it's worth noting that"
- Use corporate buzzwords listed in the Avoid section
- Write paragraphs longer than 3 sentences
- Explain theory without showing the practical application
- Use emojis as content (one max, and only if it genuinely adds)
- Start with a question ("Have you ever wondered...?")

## Anti-Patterns

Things the brand would never say, sourced from evidence:
- "Let's unpack this" — evidence: explicitly hates corporate buzzwords; this is peak LinkedIn
- "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" — evidence: anti-corporate stance Q1; this is exactly the voice he rejects
- "I'm thrilled to announce" — evidence: too performative for his understated confidence
- "Leveraging cutting-edge AI to drive transformational outcomes" — evidence: Q1 direct quote of what he despises
- "This is a game-changer" — evidence: everything is a game-changer, nothing is (his view)
- "It's important to note that" — evidence: hedging preamble, wastes reader's time
