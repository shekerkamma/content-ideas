# Brand — Body of Work

## Domain
Web scraping, data automation, Python-based workflow automation

## Core thesis

The gap between "manual and broken" and "automated and working" is about 20 minutes and 3 lines of code — but the industry makes a living pretending it's harder than that.

- The automation industry profits from complexity theater. Simple scripts solve 90% of real problems.
- If your "AI solution" is just an API call wrapped in marketing, say so. The honest version ships faster.
- The best measure of automation quality is time returned to the person, not impressiveness of the stack.

## 1. Automation is time liberation

> The purpose of automation is giving people their hours back, not building impressive systems.

- Every hour someone spends copy-pasting from a website is an hour stolen from work that actually matters.
- Automation isn't about replacing people — it's about killing the tasks that shouldn't exist in the first place.
- The ROI conversation starts at "how many hours did this give back?" not "what technology did you use?"

## 2. Simplicity beats sophistication

> The best solution is the one with the fewest moving parts that actually ships.

- If a 20-line Python script solves the problem, the framework is the wrong answer.
- "Dead simple" isn't a compromise — it's a design principle. Fewer dependencies, fewer failures.
- The best scraper is the one that actually runs. The fanciest scraper that breaks in production is worth nothing.

## 3. Show the work, skip the theory

> Credibility comes from demonstrating results, not claiming expertise.

- A screenshot of working output beats a slide deck explaining the architecture.
- "Here's what I built and how long it took" is the only content format that can't be faked.
- The tutorial IS the product. The finished thing IS the content.

## 4. The complexity theater problem

> The industry is full of people selling complexity because simplicity doesn't look impressive enough to charge for.

- "Leveraging cutting-edge AI to drive transformational outcomes" usually means "we called an API."
- Thought leadership is recycled takes in a blazer. Show code or show results.
- Enterprise tools exist to justify enterprise budgets, not to solve enterprise problems.

## 5. Laziness as engineering virtue

> The best builders are motivated by not wanting to do the same thing twice.

- The first scraper was born from not wanting to copy-paste. Every good automation starts the same way.
- "What's the shortest path to working?" is a better design question than "what's the most scalable architecture?"
- If you're doing something manually more than twice, you've already wasted time.

## 6. The anti-enterprise position

> You don't need a platform, a vendor, or a 6-month implementation. You need a script and 20 minutes.

- The corporate world teaches you what NOT to do. Then you leave and do the opposite.
- No cloud setup, no infrastructure team, no ticket system. Python, a cron job, and common sense.
- The gap between "enterprise-ready" and "actually works" is usually an extra $200K/year and 6 months of meetings.

## 7. Teaching by building

> The build IS the lesson. Don't explain how something works — show it working.

- Step-by-step walkthroughs with real code beat conceptual overviews every time.
- If you can't build it in front of someone in 20 minutes, you don't understand it well enough to teach it.
- Break complex topics into simple steps. If any step feels complicated, break it down further.
