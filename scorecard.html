1Customer▸Conversational supportMercedes MBUX, Wendy's FreshAI, Home Depot Magic ApronPer-seat help desks (Zendesk)HHLREPLACE80 support seats → 1 agent + KB

Proof — what’s actually deployed

Mercedes-Benz routes drive-time questions through its MBUX Virtual Assistant (Gemini on Vertex AI); Wendy's FreshAI takes drive-thru orders end-to-end; Home Depot's “Magic Apron” and Macy's “Ask Macy's” resolve product and order questions in-app. These are live, branded tier-1 agents already handling the bulk of routine contacts — not pilots.

SaaS in the crosshairs

The exposed layer is the per-seat help desk (Zendesk-class) plus QA-scoring tools and the lower tier of BPO. Once an agent resolves ~80% of volume, the buyer stops paying for ~80% of the agent seats and the remaining staff become escalation managers. The ticketing system of record can survive — the seat count is what collapses, which is why this is a clear Replace.

2Customer▸In-product owner assistantVolkswagen myVW, GM OnStarManuals / FAQ / deflection toolsMMMRENEGOTIATEKeep platform; drop deflection seats

Proof — what’s actually deployed

Volkswagen's myVW app lets owners ask “how do I change a flat tire?” and point a phone camera at dashboard lights for multimodal answers; GM's OnStar assistant interprets driver intent by voice. The manual and support content is embedded directly into the product experience.

SaaS in the crosshairs

This enriches more than it replaces — the value sits in proprietary product data, so the OEM keeps the platform. What erodes is spend on standalone FAQ / knowledge-base tools and call-deflection add-ons, now folded into one in-product agent. Verdict leans Renegotiate: keep the system, drop the bolt-ons.

An agent grounded in the catalog and inventory does this natively, so the per-seat tooling compresses. The commerce platform itself stays as system of record. Replace.

Volume = task throughput · Determinism = how well-defined the output · Data moat = proprietary / system-of-record strength. High volume + high determinism + low moat → Replace. High moat → Keep (agent enriches). Mixed → Renegotiate the seat model.

How to use this. Map your own SaaS line items onto these 25 archetypes, then act on the verdict: Replace → run the build-vs-buy math and pilot an agent in parallel for two weeks. Renegotiate → your renewal leverage just improved; move off per-seat toward consumption / outcome pricing. Keep → the platform is your data moat; let the agent ride on top via API / MCP. Deployments are real and named; the H/M/L scores and verdicts are structured judgment — directional guidance, not measured counts.
